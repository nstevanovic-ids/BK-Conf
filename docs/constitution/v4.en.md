# Constitution of the Balkan Confederation — Balkanska Konfederacija
## Consolidated text, version 4.11 (25 October 2026)

*Published English text of the Constitution in force. The French working text is `docs/constitution/v4.md`; the transition annex is kept there.*

---

# Preamble

We, the peoples of the Balkan Confederation, and all the communities who share this land,

United by one language of a thousand faces which, from place to place, makes us mutually intelligible from the Julian Alps to the shores of the Black Sea, and holding this mutual understanding, older than our borders and our quarrels, as the first of our common goods;

Aware that we inhabit the one crossroads of Europe where the Latin West, the Byzantine East and the Ottoman world met, overlapped and mingled on the same soil, and recognising this overlap, long lived as a rupture, as our singularity;

Having each, in our turn and under different masters, known the subjection of empires, and knowing from an experience paid for in blood that no people is free while it dominates another;

Having each, in our turn, set the same refusal against empires, and built our nations not against empires alone, but against the very idea that one people may govern another without its consent;

Remembering those among us who made war on one another, and refusing both the oblivion that betrays the dead and the rancour that condemns the living;

Resolved to found our union neither on blood, nor on a supposed common origin, nor on the domination of the most numerous, but solely on the freely renewed will to live together;

Affirming that this Confederation is not one more empire, but its exact opposite: a home one enters by choice, remains in by consent, and that no one is compelled to inhabit;

Determined to guarantee to everyone their language, their faith, their memory and their letters, and to every person the right to belong to no one but themselves;

We give ourselves this Constitution.

*Naš dom — Our home.*

# Title I / General provisions

### Article 1 / The Balkan Confederation

The seven constituent peoples and all the communities who share their land, united in the confederated States and the confederal autonomous municipalities listed in Article 1 bis, form the Balkan Confederation (Balkanska Konfederacija).

### Article 2 / Purpose

The Balkan Confederation protects the liberty and the rights of its peoples and of every person, and safeguards the independence and the security of the country.

It promotes the common prosperity, the internal cohesion and the cultural, linguistic and religious diversity of the country.

It ensures the greatest possible equality of opportunity among citizens and among States.

### Article 3 / The confederated States

The confederated States are sovereign except to the extent that their sovereignty is limited by the Confederal Constitution; they exercise all rights that are not vested in the Confederation.

### Article 4 / Languages

The national language of the Confederation is the južnoslovenski jezik. It is expressed through three regional standards, western, central and eastern, equal in dignity and in law (Article 128).

Each State determines its official language within these standards and may recognise dialect standards (Article 127).

The languages of minority communities are protected within their autonomous entities (Articles 1 quater and 127).

The Confederation ensures that dialectal diversity is represented as far as possible in territorial administration.

### Article 5 / Principles of public activity governed by the rule of law

The law is the basis and the limit of the activity of the Confederation, the States and the municipalities.

All public activity must be in the public interest and proportionate to the ends sought.

Authorities and private persons must act in good faith.

The Confederation and the States respect international law.

### Article 6 / Subsidiarity

The allocation and the performance of public tasks are based on the principle of subsidiarity.

A task is entrusted to the level closest to the citizens that is able to perform it: the municipality, then the State, then the Confederation (Article 43).

### Article 7 / Individual and social responsibility

Every person is responsible for himself and contributes, according to his abilities, to the tasks of the community and of society.

### Article 7 bis / Symbols of the Confederation

The flag of the Confederation consists of a vertical red band at the hoist and a field of three horizontal bands, blue, white and blue. It is the flag of citizens and of municipal authorities.

The state flag also bears, on the red band, a radiant golden sun charged with the winged lion gules; its white band is bordered by friezes inspired by Balkan textiles. It is reserved for the confederal institutions, the armed forces and the representations of the Confederation abroad. The law lays down the proportions and shades of both flags.

The emblem of the Confederation is the golden winged lion. The seal of the Confederation bears the winged lion on a seven-rayed sun.

The motto of the Confederation is "Naš dom" (Our home).

The anthem of the Confederation is laid down by law.

Laws concerning the symbols of the Confederation are subject to the consent of the Narodni Sabor (Article 109 a).

No major confederal institution may be concentrated in a single city. Confederal law may assign secondary institutions to other cities of the Confederation.

Sessions of the Kongres may be held in any confederal territory by decision of the Konfederalno Veće.

## Chapter 2 / Autonomous entities

### Article 1 quater / Definition and status of autonomous entities

Within certain States, autonomous entities are constituted in order to guarantee the cultural, educational and municipal rights of minority communities.

Autonomous entities hold their own competences in matters of:

a. education in the minority language and adapted school curricula;

b. cultural institutions, media and community libraries;

c. management of ordinary municipal affairs within their territorial perimeter;

d. preservation and promotion of the cultural and religious heritage of the community;

e. cross-border cooperation with kindred communities in neighbouring States.

Autonomous entities have no legislative power of their own. They exercise their competences within the framework of the laws of the State to which they are attached and of the Confederal Constitution.

Autonomous entities have a right to be consulted on any decision of the State that directly affects the interests of their community.

### Article 1 sexies / Protection of autonomous entities

The territorial limits of an autonomous entity may not be altered without the consent of the majority of its inhabitants expressed by referendum.

Every State is bound to consult the autonomous entities it hosts before adopting measures that directly affect the education, culture or municipal organisation of their communities.

Autonomous entities may address petitions directly to the Narodni Sabor when they consider that their rights are threatened.

The Confederation ensures respect for the rights of autonomous entities and may intervene in the event of a proven violation.

### Article 1 septies / Confederal autonomous municipalities

The confederal autonomous municipalities of Brčko and Mostar are directly under confederal jurisdiction. They are attached to no State.

They have a municipal council elected by direct suffrage of their inhabitants.

The composition of their municipal council guarantees fair representation of all resident communities.

The Confederation finances their essential public services.

Their status may be amended only by an amendment to the Constitution, approved in addition by a local referendum.

### Article 1 octies / Council of Communities

The Council of Communities (Veće Zajednica) represents before the confederal institutions the communities which belong to no constituent people and have autonomous entities: the Albanian, Hungarian, Slovak and Turkish communities.

Its members are appointed by the autonomous entities of each community, as provided by law. Each community holds an equal number of seats.

The Council of Communities gives an opinion on any confederal bill concerning the status of autonomous entities, the languages of their communities or education in those languages. The Kongres and the Narodni Sabor take note of that opinion before deciding.

It may bring disputes concerning the rights of autonomous entities before the Ustavni Sud (Article 118 d).

# Title II / Fundamental rights, citizenship and social goals

## Chapter 1 / Fundamental rights

### Article 8 / Human dignity

Human dignity must be respected and protected.

### Article 9 / Equality

All human beings are equal before the law.

No one may be discriminated against, in particular on grounds of origin, membership of a people or of a community, registration or non-registration in a national register, sex, age, language, regional standard or alphabet, social position, way of life, religious, philosophical or political convictions, or because of a physical, mental or psychological disability.

Men and women have equal rights. The law ensures their equality, both in law and in practice, most particularly in the family, in education and in the workplace. Men and women have the right to equal pay for work of equal value.

The law provides for the elimination of inequalities that affect persons with disabilities.

### Article 10 / Protection against arbitrary conduct and principle of good faith

Every person has the right to be treated by the authorities without arbitrariness and in good faith.

### Article 11 / Right to life and to personal liberty

Every human being has the right to life. The death penalty is prohibited.

Every human being has the right to personal liberty, and in particular to physical and mental integrity and to freedom of movement.

Torture and any other form of cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment are prohibited.

### Article 12 / Protection of children and young people

Children and young people have the right to the special protection of their integrity and to the encouragement of their development.

They exercise their rights themselves to the extent that they are capable of judgement.

### Article 13 / Right to assistance when in need

Persons in need and unable to provide for themselves have the right to assistance and care, and to the financial means required for a decent standard of living.

### Article 14 / Right to privacy

Every person has the right to privacy in his private and family life and in his home, and in relation to his correspondence and communications by post and telecommunications.

Every person has the right to be protected against the misuse of his personal data.

Data concerning the national, religious or linguistic affiliation of a person may be collected only with his consent. They serve only the purposes provided for by the Constitution and are kept separately from any other register.

### Article 15 / Right to marry and to have a family

The right to marry and to have a family is guaranteed.

### Article 16 / Freedom of conscience and belief

Freedom of conscience and belief is guaranteed.

Every person has the right to choose freely his religion or his philosophical convictions, and to profess them alone or in community with others.

Every person has the right to join or to belong to a religious community and to receive religious instruction.

No person may be forced to join or belong to a religious community, to participate in a religious act or to receive religious instruction.

### Article 17 / Freedom of opinion and information

Freedom of opinion and freedom of information are guaranteed.

Every person has the right freely to form, express and impart his opinions.

Every person has the right freely to receive information, to gather it from generally accessible sources and to disseminate it.

### Article 18 / Freedom of the media

Freedom of the press, radio and television and of other forms of dissemination of features and information by means of public telecommunications is guaranteed.

Censorship is prohibited.

The protection of sources is guaranteed.

### Article 19 / Freedom of language

Freedom of language is guaranteed.

It includes the free use of one's language, regional standard and alphabet, Latin or Cyrillic (Articles 129 and 130).

### Article 20 / Right to basic education

The right to an adequate basic education, free of charge, is guaranteed.

### Article 21 / Academic freedom

Freedom of teaching and of scientific research is guaranteed.

### Article 22 / Artistic freedom

Freedom of artistic expression is guaranteed.

### Article 23 / Freedom of assembly

Freedom of assembly is guaranteed.

Every person has the right to organise meetings and to participate or not to participate in meetings.

### Article 24 / Freedom of association

Freedom of association is guaranteed.

Every person has the right to form, join or belong to an association and to participate in its activities.

No person may be compelled to join or to belong to an association.

### Article 25 / Freedom of establishment

Citizens of the Confederation have the right to settle in any place within the confederal territory.

They have the right to leave or to enter the Confederation.

Settling in another State does not change the citizen's attachment (Article 38).

### Article 26 / Protection against expulsion, extradition and deportation

Citizens of the Confederation may not be expelled; they may be extradited to a foreign authority only with their consent.

Refugees may not be deported or extradited to a country in which they will be persecuted.

No person may be deported to a country in which he faces the threat of torture or any other form of cruel or inhuman treatment or punishment.

### Article 27 / Guarantee of ownership

The right to own property is guaranteed.

Full compensation is due in the event of expropriation or any restriction of ownership equivalent to expropriation.

### Article 28 / Economic freedom

Economic freedom is guaranteed.

It includes in particular the freedom to choose an occupation as well as the freedom to pursue a private economic activity.

### Article 29 / Freedom of association of workers and employers

Workers, employers and their organisations have the right to join together in order to protect their interests, to form associations and to join or not to join such associations.

Disputes are, wherever possible, resolved through negotiation or mediation.

Strikes and lockouts are permitted if they relate to employment relations and do not contravene any obligations to preserve peaceful employment relations or to conduct conciliation proceedings.

The law may prohibit certain categories of person from taking strike action.

### Article 30 / Guarantees in judicial proceedings

Every person whose case falls to be judged in judicial proceedings has the right to have his case heard by a legally constituted, competent, independent and impartial court. Ad hoc courts are prohibited.

Any person against whom civil proceedings have been raised has the right to have his case decided by a court within the jurisdiction in which he resides. The law may provide for a different place of jurisdiction.

Court hearings and the delivery of judgments are public. The law may provide for exceptions.

Every party may address the courts in the regional standard of his choice. Members of minority communities may address the courts of their autonomous entity in their own language (Article 127).

### Article 31 / Deprivation of liberty

No person may be deprived of his liberty other than in the circumstances and in the manner provided for by law.

Any person deprived of his liberty has the right to be notified without delay, in a language that he understands, of the reasons for his detention and of his rights. He must be given the opportunity to exercise his rights. He has in particular the right to have his next of kin notified.

Any person taken into pre-trial detention has the right to be brought before a judge without delay; the judge decides whether the person must remain in detention or be released. Every person in pre-trial detention has the right to be tried within a reasonable time.

Any person deprived of his liberty without a court order has the right to have recourse to the courts at any time. The court decides as quickly as possible on the legality of the detention.

### Article 32 / Criminal proceedings

Every person is presumed innocent until a final conviction has been pronounced.

Every accused person has the right to be notified as quickly and comprehensively as possible of the charges brought against him. He must be given the opportunity to exercise the rights of the defence.

Every convicted person has the right to have his conviction reviewed by a higher court. Cases in which the Confederal Court of Justice rules as the sole instance are reserved.

### Article 33 / Right of petition

Every person has the right to address petitions to the authorities without suffering any prejudice as a result.

The authorities must take note of petitions.

### Article 34 / Political rights

Political rights are guaranteed.

The guarantee of political rights protects the freedom of the citizen to form an opinion and to give genuine expression to his will.

### Article 35 / Upholding fundamental rights

Fundamental rights must be upheld throughout the legal system.

Whoever performs a public task is bound by fundamental rights and is under a duty to contribute to their realisation.

The authorities ensure that fundamental rights, where appropriate, apply to relationships among private persons.

Any person who considers that an authority has violated his fundamental rights may apply to the Ustavni Sud under the conditions laid down by law (Article 118).

### Article 36 / Restrictions on fundamental rights

Restrictions on fundamental rights must have a legal basis. Significant restrictions must have their basis in a law. Cases of serious and immediate danger where no other course of action is possible are exempt.

Restrictions on fundamental rights must be justified in the public interest or for the protection of the fundamental rights of others.

Any restrictions on fundamental rights must be proportionate.

The essence of fundamental rights is sacrosanct.

## Chapter 2 / Citizenship and political rights

### Article 37 / Confederal citizenship

Every person who declares his will to be a citizen and who is attached to a confederated State or to a confederal autonomous municipality is a citizen of the Confederation. Citizenship takes effect when the citizen card is issued.

Confederal citizenship is independent of registration in a national register (Article 102). It may not be made conditional on belonging to a people, a language, a regional standard, an alphabet or a religion.

No one may be favoured or disadvantaged by reason of his State of attachment.

No one may be deprived of confederal citizenship against his will. Every citizen may renounce it.

### Article 38 / Attachment

Attachment is determined by the municipality of origin of the person, of one of his parents or of one of his grandparents; failing that, by his municipality of residence within the territory of the Confederation. It is not a matter of free choice.

The municipalities of the occupied territories, claimed by a confederated State and administered by a third country, establish attachment to that State in the same way as other municipalities.

Confederal law regulates:

a. the attachment of minors by descent and by adoption;

b. the period of residence required where attachment rests on residence alone;

c. change of attachment, which may not result from free choice;

d. the keeping of the register of citizens.

### Article 39 / Exercise of political rights

The Confederation regulates the exercise of political rights at confederal level; the States regulate it at State and municipal level.

Political rights at confederal and State level are exercised in the State of attachment. No person may exercise political rights in more than one State.

Citizens attached to a confederal autonomous municipality elect the Kongres in the constituency of that municipality (Article 103).

The States may grant political rights at municipal level to persons resident in the municipality. They may provide that persons who have newly settled enjoy them only after a waiting period of no more than three months.

The election of the Narodni Sabor is reserved to citizens registered in the corresponding national register (Articles 102 and 108).

### Article 40 / Citizens living abroad and the diaspora

The Confederation encourages relations among citizens living abroad and their relations with the Confederation. It may support organisations that pursue this objective, in particular in the occupied territories.

Citizens living abroad exercise their political rights in the State to which they are attached.

Confederal law regulates the rights and obligations of citizens living abroad, in particular the exercise of political rights at confederal level, the fulfilment of military service and alternative service, assistance to persons in need and social insurance.

## Chapter 3 / Social goals

### Article 41 / Social goals

The Confederation and the States, as a complement to personal responsibility and private initiative, endeavour to ensure that:

a. every person has access to social security;

b. every person has access to the health care that he requires;

c. families are protected and encouraged as communities of adults and children;

d. every person capable of working can earn his living by working under fair conditions;

e. every person seeking accommodation can find, for himself and his family, appropriate accommodation on reasonable terms;

f. children and young people, as well as persons of working age, can obtain an education and undergo basic and advanced training in accordance with their abilities;

g. children and young people are encouraged to become independent and socially responsible people and are supported in their social, cultural and political integration.

The Confederation and the States endeavour to ensure that every person is protected against the economic consequences of old age, invalidity, illness, accident, unemployment, maternity, being orphaned and being widowed.

They endeavour to achieve these social goals within the scope of their constitutional powers and the resources available to them.

No direct right to State benefits may be established on the basis of these social goals.

# Title III / The Confederation, the States and the municipalities

## Chapter 1 / Relations between the Confederation and the confederated States

#### Section 1 / Tasks of the Confederation and of the confederated States

### Article 42 / Tasks of the Confederation

The Confederation fulfils the tasks that are assigned to it by the Constitution.

### Article 43 / Tasks of the States and allocation of tasks

The confederated States decide on the tasks that they are to fulfil within the scope of their powers.

The Confederation undertakes only those tasks that the States are unable to perform or which require uniform regulation by the Confederation.

The collective body that benefits from a public service bears its costs. The collective body that bears the costs of a public service decides on the nature of that service.

Basic services must be available to every person in a comparable manner. Financial equalisation provides for this (Article 131).

The tasks of the Confederation must be fulfilled economically and in accordance with requirements.

#### Section 2 / Cooperation between the Confederation and the confederated States

### Article 44 / Principles

The Confederation and the States support each other in the fulfilment of their tasks and cooperate with each other.

They owe each other a duty of consideration and support. They provide each other with administrative assistance and mutual judicial assistance.

Disputes between States, or between the States and the Confederation, are wherever possible resolved by negotiation or mediation. Failing that, the Ustavni Sud decides (Article 118).

### Article 45 / Participation in confederal decision-making

The States participate, in the cases provided for by the Constitution, in confederal decision-making, and in particular in the legislative process (Articles 106 bis and 122).

The Confederation informs the States of its intentions in good time and in full. It consults the States where their interests are affected.

### Article 46 / Implementation of confederal law

The States implement confederal law in accordance with the Constitution and the law.

The Confederation and the States may together agree that the States should achieve specific goals in the implementation of confederal law and may to this end conduct programmes that receive financial support from the Confederation.

The Confederation allows the States all possible discretion to organise their own affairs and takes account of their particularities.

### Article 47 / Autonomy of the States

The Confederation respects the autonomy of the States.

It leaves the States sufficient tasks of their own and respects their organisational autonomy. It leaves the States with sufficient sources of finance and contributes towards ensuring that they have the financial resources required to fulfil their tasks.

### Article 48 / Inter-Balkan agreements

The States may enter into agreements with each other and establish common organisations and institutions. In particular, they may jointly undertake tasks of regional or local importance.

The Confederation may participate in such organisations or institutions within the scope of its powers.

Inter-Balkan agreements must not be contrary to the law or to the interests of the Confederation or to the law of other States. The Confederation must be notified of such agreements.

The States may by inter-Balkan agreement authorise a common body to issue legislative provisions for the implementation of that agreement, provided that the agreement:

a. is approved in each State party in accordance with the procedure that applies to its laws;

b. lays down the essential features of those provisions.

The States respect inter-Balkan law.

### Article 49 / Declaration of general application and requirement to participate

At the request of interested States, the Confederation may declare inter-Balkan agreements to be generally binding or require States to participate in inter-Balkan agreements in the following areas:

a. the execution of criminal penalties and measures;

b. school education, in the matters specified in Article 64;

c. higher education institutions (Article 66);

d. cultural institutions of supra-regional importance;

e. waste management;

f. waste water treatment;

g. urban transport;

h. advanced medical science and specialist clinics;

i. institutions for the rehabilitation and care of persons with disabilities.

The declaration of general application and the requirement to participate are made by the Kongres in the form of a confederal decree.

The law specifies the requirements for the declaration of general application and for the requirement to participate, and regulates the procedure.

### Article 50 / Precedence of and compliance with confederal law

Confederal law takes precedence over any conflicting provision of the law of the States.

The Confederation ensures that the States comply with confederal law.

### Article 50 bis / Confederal enforcement

Where a State fails to comply with confederal law, the Konfederalno Veće gives it formal notice and sets a reasonable time limit for compliance. The State may apply to the Ustavni Sud.

When the time limit has expired, the Konfederalno Veće may apply to the Ustavni Sud. If the Court finds the failure, the Confederation may:

a. carry out, at the expense of the State, the acts that the State refuses to carry out;

b. suspend all or part of the confederal contributions paid to the State, except the equalisation needed to keep its resources at the floor laid down in Article 132 b.

Any coercive measure requires the approval of the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations. Force may be used only within a state of emergency (Article 146).

The measures end as soon as the State complies with confederal law.

#### Section 3 / Municipalities

### Article 51 / Municipalities

The municipalities (opštine) are the basic communities of the Confederation. The municipality of origin establishes the attachment of citizens (Article 38).

The autonomy of the municipalities is guaranteed in accordance with the law of the State.

The Confederation takes account in its activities of their possible consequences for the municipalities. In doing so, it takes account of the special position of cities, urban areas and mountain regions.

Municipalities may form associations. Associations of municipalities recognised as autonomous entities are governed by Articles 1 quater to 1 sexies.

#### Section 4 / Confederal guarantees

### Article 52 / Constitutions of the States

Each State adopts a democratic constitution. This requires the approval of the people of the State and must be capable of being revised if the majority of those eligible to vote so request.

The constitution of every State guarantees the rights of the autonomous entities and of the communities established on its territory.

The constitutions of the States require the guarantee of the Confederation. The Kongres grants the guarantee if the constitution is not contrary to confederal law.

### Article 53 / Constitutional order

The Confederation protects the constitutional order of the States.

It intervenes, at the request of the State concerned, where order in that State is disrupted or threatened and the State is unable to preserve it alone or with the help of other States (Article 139).

### Article 54 / Existence, status and territory of the States

The Confederation protects the existence and status of the States, as well as their territory.

Any change in the number of States or in their status requires the consent of the citizens and of the States concerned, together with the approval of the people and the States.

Any change in the territory of a State requires the consent of the citizens and of the States concerned; it then requires the approval of the Kongres in the form of a confederal decree and the consent of the Narodni Sabor (Article 109).

Boundary adjustments between States are made by agreement between the States concerned.

## Chapter 2 / Powers

#### Section 1 / Relations with foreign states

### Article 55 / Foreign relations

Foreign relations are the responsibility of the Confederation.

The Confederation strives to preserve the independence and prosperity of the country; it contributes in particular to the relief of need and poverty and to the promotion of respect for human rights and democracy, the peaceful co-existence of peoples and the conservation of natural resources.

It takes account of the powers of the States and protects their interests.

### Article 56 / Participation of the States in foreign policy decisions

The confederated States are consulted on foreign policy decisions that affect their powers or their essential interests.

The Confederation informs the States in good time and in full and consults them.

The views of the States are of particular importance if their powers are affected. In such cases, the States participate in international negotiations in an appropriate manner.

### Article 57 / Relations between the States and foreign states

The confederated States may conclude treaties with foreign states on matters that lie within the scope of their powers.

Such treaties must not conflict with the law or the interests of the Confederation or with the law of other States. Before concluding a treaty, the States must inform the Confederation.

The States may deal directly with lower-ranking foreign authorities; in other cases, the relations of the States with foreign states are conducted through the Confederation.

#### Section 2 / Security, national defence, civil defence

### Article 58 / Security

The Confederation and the States ensure the security of the country and the protection of the population within the scope of their respective powers.

They coordinate their efforts in the area of internal security.

### Article 59 / Armed forces

The Balkan Confederation has armed forces, the Konfederalne Oružane Snage. In principle, the armed forces are organised as a militia.

The armed forces serve to prevent war and to maintain peace; they defend the country and its population.

They support the civilian authorities in averting serious threats to internal security and in dealing with other exceptional situations. The law may provide for further duties.

The deployment of the armed forces is the responsibility of the Confederation.

### Article 60 / Military service and alternative service

Every male citizen of the Confederation is required to do military service. The law provides for alternative civilian service.

Citizens of the Confederation may serve in the armed forces on a voluntary basis, regardless of their sex.

Every male citizen who does not do military service or alternative service owes a tax. This tax is levied by the Confederation and assessed and collected by the confederated States.

The Confederation legislates on fair compensation for loss of income arising from military service or alternative service.

Persons whose health is damaged while doing military service or alternative service are entitled to appropriate support from the Confederation, for themselves or for their next of kin. If they lose their lives, their next of kin are entitled to similar support.

### Article 61 / Organisation, training and equipment of the armed forces

Legislation on the armed forces and the organisation, training and equipment of the armed forces are the responsibility of the Confederation.

The Confederation may take over military installations of the confederated States in return for fair compensation.

### Article 61 bis / Command of the armed forces

The armed forces are placed under the authority of the Konfederalno Veće, which exercises it through the Department of Defence and Security.

They are commanded by the Chief of the General Staff (načelnik Generalštaba). He is proposed by the Konfederalno Veće and confirmed by the Kongres by a two-thirds majority and by the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations, for a term of four years renewable once. The outgoing holder remains in office until his successor takes office.

The officer corps of the armed forces reflects the diversity of the population of the Confederation. No people may be durably over-represented in their high command.

When the deployment of the armed forces for the defence of the country is authorised (Article 105 d), the Kongres and the Narodni Sabor, meeting as the Confederal Assembly, elect a commander-in-chief, who holds the rank of general for the duration of the deployment. The Chief of the General Staff is subordinate to him.

### Article 62 / Civil defence

Legislation on civil defence is the responsibility of the Confederation; civil defence, under the direction of the Confederal Agency for Balkan Emergency Management (ACGUB; Konfederalna Agencija za Vanredne Situacije), has the task of protecting persons and property in the event of armed conflict.

The Confederation legislates on the deployment of civil defence units in the event of disasters and emergencies; the ACGUB directs such deployments.

It may declare civil defence service compulsory for men. Women may volunteer.

The Confederation legislates on fair compensation for loss of income arising from civil defence service.

Persons whose health is damaged while doing civil defence service are entitled to appropriate support from the Confederation, for themselves or for their next of kin. If they lose their lives, their next of kin are entitled to similar support.

#### Section 3 / Education, research and culture

### Article 63 / Balkan Education Area (EBF)

Within the scope of their powers, the Confederation and the States jointly ensure a high level of quality in and accessibility to the Balkan Education Area (EBF).

They coordinate their efforts and ensure their cooperation through joint executive bodies and by other means.

In fulfilling their duties, they endeavour to ensure that general education and vocational education and training are equally recognised by society.

### Article 64 / School education

School education is a matter for the confederated States.

The confederated States provide for adequate basic education that is available to all children. Basic education is compulsory and subject to state direction or supervision. At public schools, it is free of charge.

The confederated States provide adequate special needs education to all children and young people with disabilities up to the age of twenty at the latest.

If coordination does not result in the harmonisation of school education in respect of the compulsory school age, the age of starting school, the duration and objectives of the levels of education and the transition from one level to another, and the recognition of qualifications, the Confederation legislates to the extent necessary.

The Confederation regulates the beginning of the school year.

The confederated States participate in the preparation of confederal acts that affect their powers; their opinions carry particular weight.

### Article 65 / Vocational education and training

The Confederation legislates on vocational education and training.

It promotes diversity and accessibility in vocational education and training.

### Article 66 / Higher education institutions

The Confederation runs the confederal polytechnic schools (KPU). It may establish, take over or run other higher education institutions and other institutions of higher education.

It supports the higher education institutions of the confederated States and may make financial contributions to other institutions of higher education recognised by it.

The Confederation and the States jointly ensure the coordination and quality assurance of the Balkan higher education area. In doing so, they take account of the autonomy of the higher education institutions and of the different bodies responsible for them, and ensure the equal treatment of institutions with the same functions.

In order to fulfil their duties, the Confederation and the States enter into agreements and delegate specific powers to joint bodies. The law specifies the powers that may be delegated to such bodies and lays down the principles applicable to the organisation of and procedure for coordination.

If the Confederation and the States fail to achieve their common objectives by means of coordination, the Confederation legislates on the levels of study and on the transition from one level to another, on continuing education and on the recognition of institutions and qualifications. In addition, the Confederation may make the funding of higher education institutions subject to uniform funding principles, and dependent on the sharing of tasks between the institutions in areas of particularly high costs.

### Article 67 / Research

The Confederation promotes scientific research and innovation.

It may make its support conditional in particular on quality assurance and coordination being guaranteed.

It may run, establish or take over research institutes.

### Article 68 / Continuing education and training

The Confederation lays down principles on continuing education and training.

It may promote continuing education and training.

The law specifies the areas and the criteria.

### Article 69 / Statistics

The Confederation collects the statistical data required on the status and development of the population, the economy, society, education, research, the territory and the Balkan environment.

It may legislate on the harmonisation and maintenance of official registers in order to rationalise the collection of data.

### Article 70 / Education grants

The Confederation may contribute to the confederated States' expenditure on education grants awarded to students at higher education and other institutions of higher education. It may promote the harmonisation of education grants between the States and lay down principles for their award.

In addition, it may, in supplementing the measures taken by the confederated States and while respecting the autonomy of the States in matters of school education, take its own measures to promote education.

### Article 71 / Children and young people

In fulfilling their duties, the Confederation and the confederated States take account of the special need of children and young people for development and protection.

In supplementing measures taken by the confederated States, the Confederation may support out-of-school work with children and young people.

### Article 72 / Music education

The Confederation and the confederated States support music education, in particular for children and young people.

Within the scope of their powers, the Confederation and the confederated States are committed to promoting high-quality music teaching in schools. If the efforts of the confederated States do not result in the harmonisation of the objectives of music teaching in schools, the Confederation legislates to the extent necessary.

The Confederation, with the participation of the confederated States, lays down principles to ensure that young people have access to music-making and that musically talented persons are encouraged.

The Confederation and the confederated States also undertake to preserve and promote the music of the Balkans and ancient musical traditions. They support initiatives to integrate this cultural heritage into educational programmes and out-of-school activities, in order to strengthen the cultural identity and musical diversity of the Balkan Confederation.

### Article 73 / Sport

The Confederation encourages sport, in particular training in sport at every level, recognising its importance for the health, well-being and social development of citizens.

It runs several confederal sports institutes, similar to the polytechnic schools but specialising in elite sport. These institutes, called Konfederalni Politehnički Sportski Instituti (KPSI), are dedicated to training elite athletes and developing sporting talent. Each confederated State may host one or more KPSI according to local needs and sporting specialities.

The Confederation, in collaboration with the confederated States, ensures that the KPSI offer diversified training programmes covering a wide range of sporting disciplines, and that they are equipped with the infrastructure and resources required to achieve sporting excellence.

It may legislate on sport for young people and declare the teaching of sport in schools compulsory, thereby guaranteeing fair access to sports training for all children and young people.

The Confederation also supports programmes promoting participation in sport at every level, including mass sport, amateur sport and school sport, in order to foster an inclusive and dynamic sporting culture throughout the Balkan Confederation.

The Confederation may provide financial and logistical support for national and international sporting events, thereby encouraging the organisation of high-level competitions and the promotion of the values of sport.

Each KPSI is identified by an additional letter designating the city in which it is located: the Konfederalni Politehnički Sportski Institut of Beograd is identified as KPSI-B, that of Sarajevo as KPSI-S and that of Zagreb as KPSI-Z. The law assigns the letters of the other institutes.

### Article 74 / Culture

Culture is a matter for the confederated States.

The Confederation may support cultural activities of national interest and promote artistic and musical expression, in particular through the promotion of education and training.

In fulfilling its duties, it takes account of the cultural and linguistic diversity of the country.

The Confederation also supports the preservation and promotion of the cultural traditions and historical heritage of the Balkans, ensuring that the diverse cultural legacies are represented and valued in all cultural initiatives.

The Confederal Library and Archives (Konfederalna Biblioteka i Arhiv), established in Cetiñe, collect and preserve the written heritage of the Confederation and the archives of its institutions.

### Article 75 / Languages

The national language of the Confederation is the južnoslovenski jezik, with its three regional standards: the western standard (Slovene speech), the central standard (Štokavian speech) and the eastern standard (Bulgarian and Macedonian speech) (Article 128).

The confederated States determine their official languages within the regional standards (Article 127). In order to preserve harmony between linguistic communities, the States respect the traditional territorial distribution of languages and take account of indigenous linguistic minorities.

The Confederation and the confederated States encourage understanding and exchange between the linguistic communities.

The Confederation supports the plurilingual confederated States in the fulfilment of their special duties.

The Confederation supports measures by the confederated States to preserve and promote local language varieties and the cultural traditions associated with them.

### Article 76 / Film

The Confederation may promote Balkan film production and film culture.

It may legislate to promote a varied and high-quality range of cinematographic works.

The Confederation supports initiatives that showcase the traditions and stories of the various regions of the Balkan Confederation through film, thereby fostering cultural diversity and national identity.

### Article 77 / Religion and the State

The regulation of the relationship between religious communities and the State is the responsibility of the confederated States.

The Confederation has no official religion. The confederated States treat religious communities equally; where they recognise some of them, they do so on the same criteria for all.

The Confederation and the confederated States may within the scope of their powers take measures to preserve public peace and harmonious coexistence between the members of the various religious communities.

The Confederation guarantees the freedom to build places of worship for all religious communities, in accordance with the local laws and town-planning regulations of the confederated States.

The Confederation establishes an Interfaith Council. It brings together, in equal numbers, representatives of the Catholic, Islamic, Jewish, Orthodox and Protestant communities; the law may admit other religious communities established in the Confederation. It deliberates by consensus. The council's mission is to promote interreligious dialogue, to advise the Konfederalno Veće on religious matters and to propose initiatives to strengthen understanding and cooperation between the religious communities.

#### Section 4 / Environment and spatial planning

### Article 78 / Sustainable development

The Confederation and the confederated States endeavour to achieve a balanced and sustainable relationship between nature and its capacity to renew itself and the demands placed on it by the population.

### Article 79 / Protection of the environment

The Confederation legislates on the protection of the population and its natural environment against damage or nuisance.

It ensures that such damage or nuisance is avoided. The costs of avoiding or eliminating such damage or nuisance are borne by those responsible for causing it.

The confederated States are responsible for the implementation of the relevant confederal regulations, except where the law reserves this duty for the Confederation.

### Article 80 / Spatial planning

The Confederation lays down principles on spatial planning. Spatial planning is the responsibility of the confederated States and serves to ensure the appropriate and economic use of the land and its properly ordered settlement.

The Confederation encourages and coordinates the efforts of the confederated States and cooperates with them.

In fulfilling their duties, the Confederation and the confederated States take account of the requirements of spatial planning.

### Article 81 / Surveying

The national survey is the responsibility of the Confederation.

The Confederation legislates on official surveying.

It may legislate on the harmonisation of official land information.

### Article 82 / Second homes

The proportion of second homes in the total stock of residential units and the residential gross floor area used in any municipality is restricted to a maximum of twenty per cent.

The law requires municipalities to publish their primary residence quota plan and a detailed report on its implementation every year.

This article enters into force progressively. Municipalities have a period of five years from the entry into force of this Constitution to comply with the second-home quotas. During this period, transitional measures may be put in place to ease the gradual adaptation to the new rules.

### Article 83 / Water

The Confederation, within the scope of its powers, ensures the economic use and the protection of water resources and provides protection against the harmful effects of water.

It lays down principles on the conservation and exploitation of water resources, the use of water for the production of energy and for cooling purposes, and on other measures affecting the water cycle.

It legislates on the protection of waters, on ensuring appropriate residual water flows, on hydraulic engineering, on dam safety and on measures to influence precipitation.

The confederated States manage their water resources. They may levy charges for the use of water, subject to the limits imposed by confederal legislation. The Confederation has the right to use water for its transport undertakings; in such cases, it pays a charge and compensation.

In consultation with the confederated States concerned, the Confederation decides on rights to water resources shared with third countries and on the charges for the use of such resources. It also decides on such rights where water resources concern several confederated States and these States cannot reach agreement.

In fulfilling its duties, the Confederation takes account of the interests of the confederated States from which the water originates.

### Article 84 / Forests

The Confederation ensures that the forests are able to fulfil their protective, commercial and public amenity functions.

It lays down principles on the protection of the forests.

It encourages measures for the conservation of the forests.

### Article 85 / Protection of nature, heritage and maritime areas

The protection of nature, of heritage, of maritime areas and of national parks is a matter for the confederated States.

In the fulfilment of its duties, the Confederation takes account of the objectives of the protection of nature, heritage, maritime areas and national parks. It protects landscapes, the character of localities, historic sites and natural and cultural monuments, as well as coastlines and islands; it preserves them in their entirety if this is required by public interest.

It may support efforts directed towards the protection of nature, heritage, maritime areas and national parks, and may, by contract or compulsory purchase, acquire or secure items of national importance.

It legislates on the protection of fauna and flora and of marine, coastal and terrestrial ecosystems, and on the preservation of their natural habitats in all their diversity. It protects endangered species from extinction.

Moors, wetlands of particular beauty, protected maritime areas, national parks and islands of national importance are protected. No buildings may be constructed in them and no alterations may be made to the land. Installations that serve to protect these areas or to continue their use for agricultural and conservation purposes are excepted.

The Confederation also ensures the preservation of maritime resources and the protection of territorial waters against pollution and harm to the marine environment.

The Confederation encourages and coordinates the management of national parks in collaboration with the confederated States, in order to guarantee their conservation and public access while preserving their ecological integrity.

### Article 86 / Fishing and hunting

The Confederation lays down principles on fishing and hunting, and in particular on preserving the diversity of fish species, wild mammals and birds.

### Article 87 / Protection of animals

The Confederation legislates on the protection of animals.

It regulates in particular:

a. the keeping and care of animals;

b. experiments on animals and procedures carried out on living animals;

c. the use of animals;

d. the import of animals and animal products;

e. the trade in animals and the transport of animals;

f. the slaughter of animals.

The confederated States are responsible for the implementation of the relevant confederal regulations, except where the law reserves this duty for the Confederation.

#### Section 5 / Public works and transport

### Article 88 / Public works

In the interest of the country or a large part of the country, the Confederation may build and operate public works or support their construction.

### Article 89 / Public transport

The Confederation is responsible for setting the main lines of inter-State transport policy, including road, rail, maritime and air infrastructure, in collaboration with the confederated States, and ensures the coordination required for the proper functioning of the overall network. The details of funding and operational management are defined by specific laws.

The Confederation and the confederated States ensure that an adequate range of public transport services by rail, road, inland waterway, sea and cableway is provided in all regions of the country. In doing so, they take due account of rail and maritime freight.

The prices paid by the users of public transport cover an appropriate share of the costs.

### Article 90 / Road traffic

The Confederation legislates on road traffic.

It exercises oversight over roads of national importance; it may determine which transit roads must remain open to traffic.

The use of public roads is free of charge. The Kongres may authorise exceptions.

### Article 91 / Road infrastructure

The Confederation and the confederated States ensure that adequate road infrastructure is available in all regions of the country.

The Confederation ensures the creation of a network of confederal roads and that they may be used. It builds, maintains and operates the confederal roads. It bears the costs. It may assign this task wholly or partly to public, private or joint organisations.

### Article 91 bis / Classification and designation of roads

Classified roads fall into two categories:

a. confederal roads, which touch the territory of at least two States, together with all classified roads situated on the territory of the confederal autonomous municipalities;

b. State roads, which remain on the territory of a single State.

A confederal road is designated by the letters Bk, a hyphen and a number without a leading zero (Bk-1, Bk-27). It bears the names of its two terminus towns (Zagreb–Sofija Road). Numbers 1 to 9 are reserved for the trunk routes linking the capitals of the Confederation (Article 1 ter); higher numbers designate the secondary network.

A State road is designated by the two letters of its State's code, the second in lower case, a hyphen and a number without a leading zero (Da-1, Su-14). It bears the name of the town at which it ends (Zadar Road). Each State numbers its roads independently.

The lower-case second letter distinguishes the designation of a road from the code of a State or of a confederal autonomous municipality (Article 1 bis): Bk-7 designates a confederal road, BK-95 the confederal autonomous municipality of Brčko.

The network of confederal roads is determined by the Kongres in the form of a confederal decree. Each State determines the list of its State roads.

### Article 92 / Heavy vehicle charge

The Confederation may levy a performance-related or consumption-related charge on heavy vehicle traffic if such traffic causes costs to the general public that are not covered by other charges or fees.

The net revenue from the charge is used to cover the costs related to land transport.

The confederated States are entitled to a share of the net revenue. In calculating their shares, the special consequences of levying the charge for mountain regions, coastal regions, islands and peripheral regions must be taken into account.

### Article 93 / Charge for the use of confederal roads

The Confederation levies a charge, in the form of a vignette, for the use of the confederal roads by motor vehicles and trailers that are not subject to the heavy vehicle charge.

### Article 94 / Fund for confederal roads and transport infrastructure

The funding of the confederal roads and of contributions to measures to improve transport infrastructure in cities and conurbations that is related to road traffic is provided by a fund.

The fund is financed from the following resources:

a. the net revenue from the charge for the use of confederal roads under Article 93;

b. the net revenue from the special consumption tax on motor fuels;

c. the net revenue from the surcharge on motor vehicles;

d. the net revenue from charges related to the use of road infrastructure;

e. a share of the net revenue from the consumption tax levied on all motor fuels other than aviation fuels; this share corresponds to nine per cent of the resources specified in letter c and to nine per cent of half of the net revenue from the consumption tax levied on all motor fuels other than aviation fuels, but at most sixty billion dinars per year; its indexation is regulated by law;

f. as a rule, ten per cent of the net revenue from the consumption tax levied on all motor fuels other than aviation fuels;

g. revenue from the special financing and the contributions of the confederated States to compensate for the additional expenditure arising from the inclusion of new sections in the confederal road network;

h. other resources allocated by law that are related to road traffic.

A special financing account is kept for the following tasks and expenditure related to road traffic:

a. contributions to measures promoting combined transport and the transport of accompanied motor vehicles;

b. contributions to the costs of State roads;

c. contributions to protective structures against natural hazards and to measures for environmental and landscape protection made necessary by road traffic;

d. general contributions to the costs incurred by the confederated States for roads open to motor vehicles;

e. contributions to confederated States without confederal roads;

f. research and administration;

g. contributions to the fund.

Half of the net revenue from the consumption tax levied on all motor fuels other than aviation fuels is credited to the special financing, after deduction of the resources specified in the second paragraph, letter e.

If there is a proven need in the special financing and in order to build up appropriate reserves within it, the revenue from the consumption tax may be credited to the special financing instead of being allocated to the fund.

### Article 95 / Railways and other modes of transport

Legislation on rail transport, cableways, maritime and inland navigation, and aviation is the responsibility of the Confederation.

The Confederation ensures that transport infrastructure is developed and maintained so as to promote connectivity between the various regions, including mountain, coastal and island areas.

The Confederation encourages environmentally friendly modes of transport and promotes the integration of sustainable transport technologies into national infrastructure.

#### Section 6 / Energy and communications

### Article 96 / Energy and communications

Within the scope of their powers, the Confederation and the confederated States endeavour to ensure a sufficient, diverse, safe, economic and environmentally sustainable energy supply as well as the economic and efficient use of energy.

The Confederation lays down principles on the use of local and renewable energy sources. It legislates on the transport and supply of electricity and gas, and on nuclear energy.

Legislation on postal services and telecommunications is the responsibility of the Confederation. It ensures that an adequate universal service is provided in all regions of the country.

Legislation on radio and television is the responsibility of the Confederation. Radio and television contribute to education and cultural development, and to the free shaping of public opinion. They take account of the diversity of the country and give a place to the three regional standards and to the languages of minority communities. Their independence and autonomy in programming are guaranteed.

#### Section 7 / Economy and currency

### Article 97 / Economy, currency and customs

The Confederation and the confederated States abide by the principle of economic freedom (Article 28). They jointly safeguard the interests of the Balkan economy.

The Confederation ensures the creation of a single Balkan economic area. It legislates on competition, consumer protection, banks and insurance, and foreign trade.

The currency is the responsibility of the Confederation. The monetary unit of the Confederation is the dinar. The Confederal Central Bank (Article 1 ter), as an independent central bank, pursues a monetary policy that serves the overall interests of the country; its primary objective is price stability.

Customs duties are the responsibility of the Confederation. It may also levy a consumption tax on motor fuels and a surcharge on motor vehicles, the revenue of which is allocated in accordance with Article 94.

The Confederation takes measures in favour of agriculture, of mountain, coastal, island and peripheral regions, and of the balanced development of the entire territory (Articles 131 to 136).

### Article 97 bis / Confederal Central Bank

The Confederal Central Bank (Konfederalna Centralna Banka) is directed by a governing board of five members, chosen for their competence in monetary and financial matters.

The members of the governing board are proposed by the Konfederalno Veće and elected by the Kongres by a two-thirds majority for a non-renewable term of seven years. Their terms are staggered as provided by law. The outgoing member remains in office until his successor takes office.

The governing board may not include more than two members belonging to the same constituent people.

The members of the governing board may receive instructions from no organ of the Confederation, no State and no national delegation. They may be removed only for serious misconduct or incapacity, by decision of two thirds of the Kongres.

#### Section 8 / Housing, employment, social security and health

### Article 98 / Housing, employment, social security and health

The Confederation encourages the construction of housing and home ownership.

The Confederation may legislate on the protection of employees, on relations between employers and employees and on public employment services. It may declare collective employment agreements to be generally binding.

The Confederation legislates on old-age, survivors' and invalidity insurance, on unemployment insurance, on health and accident insurance and on maternity insurance; it may declare such insurance compulsory. Family allowances and parental insurance are governed by Article 98 bis. These insurances implement the social goals of Article 41.

Within the scope of their powers, the Confederation and the confederated States ensure sufficient, accessible and high-quality primary medical care. The Confederation legislates on the protection of health, in particular on foodstuffs, therapeutic products and chemicals, on combating communicable diseases and on protection against ionising radiation.

### Article 98 bis / Family and birth rate

The Confederation and the States encourage births and support families.

The Confederation pays a family allowance for each child up to the age of eighteen, or twenty-five for a child in education or training. The allowance is increased from the third child.

Confederal parental insurance compensates one year of parental leave at no less than eighty per cent of previous income, within the limits set by law. Part of the leave is reserved for each parent.

The States guarantee a childcare place for every child from the age of one; the Confederation contributes to its funding.

The Confederation encourages access to housing for young families (Article 98). The confederal direct tax provides a deduction for each dependent child (Article 99 quater).

No family benefit may depend on the national affiliation, the enrolment in a national register or the religion of the parents.

#### Section 9 / Civil law, criminal law, metrology and foreign nationals

### Article 99 / Civil law, criminal law, metrology and residence of foreign nationals

Legislation in the field of civil law and the law of civil procedure is the responsibility of the Confederation.

Legislation in the field of criminal law and the law of criminal procedure is the responsibility of the Confederation. The prosecution of the offences listed in Article 141 is the responsibility of the Confederation.

The organisation of the courts, the administration of justice and the execution of penalties and measures are the responsibility of the confederated States, unless the law provides otherwise. The Confederation and the States ensure that victims of crime receive assistance.

Legislation on metrology is the responsibility of the Confederation.

Legislation on the entry, exit, residence and settlement of foreign nationals and on the granting of asylum is the responsibility of the Confederation. Foreign nationals residing in the Confederation may acquire citizenship through attachment by residence (Article 38).

The Confederation manages the immigration of foreign nationals autonomously. The number of residence permits issued for gainful employment is limited by annual quotas, which the law sets according to the needs of the economy and with priority, for equal qualifications, for citizens of the Confederation, including those living abroad.

Asylum applications lodged at the border are examined under a border procedure, within a time limit set by law. Persons whose application is rejected are returned, subject to Article 26.

The Confederation encourages the return of citizens living abroad (Article 40).

## Chapter 3 / Financial regime

### Article 99 bis / Financial management

The Confederation balances its expenditure and revenue over time.

The ceiling for the total expenditure that may be approved in the budget is set according to the estimated revenue, taking account of the economic situation.

Exceptional financial requirements may justify an appropriate increase in the ceiling. The Kongres decides on any such increase by an absolute majority of its members.

If the total expenditure in the accounts of the Confederation exceeds the ceiling, the additional expenditure is compensated for in the following years.

The details are regulated by law. The Confederal Court of Audit verifies each year that this Article is complied with (Article 136 bis).

### Article 99 ter / Principles of taxation

The Confederation levies only the taxes assigned to it by the Constitution. Other taxes belong to the States and, as their law provides, to the municipalities (Article 3).

The essential elements of each tax, in particular the group of taxpayers, the object of the tax and its assessment, are regulated by law.

The principles of universality, equality of treatment and taxation according to economic capacity must be respected. No tax may be assessed or varied according to national affiliation or enrolment in a national register.

Double taxation between States is prohibited. The Confederation takes the necessary measures.

The maximum rates set in this Chapter may be raised only by a revision of the Constitution (Title VIII).

### Article 99 quater / Direct taxes

The Confederation may levy a direct tax:

a. of no more than ten per cent on the income of natural persons;

b. of no more than eight per cent on the net profit of legal persons.

It takes account of the burden of the direct taxes levied by the States and the municipalities.

The income tax provides a deduction for each dependent child (Article 98 bis). The law may, for a limited period, reduce the tax of citizens living abroad who return to settle in the Confederation.

The tax is assessed and collected by the States. At least one quarter of its gross revenue is allocated to them; the law may assign part of this share to financial equalisation (Article 132).

The Confederation levies a top-up tax ensuring that large groups of companies bear the minimum taxation laid down by international standards. At least three quarters of its revenue is allocated to the States where the profits are made.

### Article 99 quinquies / Tax harmonisation

The Confederation lays down the principles for the harmonisation of the direct taxes of the Confederation, the States and the municipalities; it takes account of the efforts of the States towards harmonisation.

Harmonisation extends to tax liability, the object of the tax and the period of assessment, procedural law and the law on tax offences. Tax scales, tax rates and tax-free allowances are not subject to harmonisation.

The Confederation may legislate to prevent unjustified tax advantages. The harmonisation law may provide relief for research and innovation, in accordance with international standards.

### Article 99 sexies / Value added tax

The Confederation may levy a value added tax on the supply of goods and services, including supplies to oneself, and on imports, at a standard rate of no more than eighteen per cent and a reduced rate of no more than eight per cent.

The reduced rate applies to foodstuffs, medicines, books and the press, and to other goods and services of basic necessity designated by law.

Thirty per cent of the net revenue of the tax is allocated to the States and to the confederal autonomous municipalities in proportion to their population.

The States and the municipalities may levy no general tax on turnover or consumption.

### Article 99 septies / Other confederal taxes

In addition to customs duties and the taxes provided for in Article 97, the Confederation may levy:

a. special consumption taxes on tobacco and alcoholic beverages;

b. a stamp duty on insurance premiums;

c. a withholding tax of no more than fifteen per cent on income from movable capital, except bond interest, and on lottery winnings and insurance benefits; ten per cent of its revenue is allocated to the States.

Neither the Confederation nor the States levy any duty on the issue or trading of securities.

Whatever confederal legislation subjects to value added tax, to a special consumption tax, to stamp duty or to withholding tax, or declares exempt, may not be subjected by the States or the municipalities to a tax of the same kind.

# Title IV / Institutions of the Confederation

## Chapter 1 / General provisions

### Article 100 / Organs of the Confederation

The organs of the Confederation are:

a. the Kongres (legislative organ);

b. the Narodni Sabor (Assembly of Nations);

c. the Konfederalno Veće (Confederal Council — executive organ);

d. the Ustavni Sud (Constitutional Court — judicial organ).

### Article 101 / Separation of powers

The legislative, executive and judicial functions are separate.

No one may sit simultaneously in more than one confederal organ.

Members of the Konfederalno Veće may hold no other public office nor pursue any private gainful activity during their term.

### Article 101 bis / Immunities

Members of the Kongres, the Narodni Sabor and the Konfederalno Veće, and the Chancellor of the Confederation, bear no legal liability for the votes they cast and the statements they make before the chambers and their organs. This immunity may be neither lifted nor waived.

Criminal proceedings against a member of the Kongres, the Narodni Sabor or the Konfederalno Veće for an offence directly connected with his functions require the prior authorisation of the special chamber of the Ustavni Sud.

During sessions, no member of the Kongres or of the Narodni Sabor may be prosecuted or deprived of liberty for an offence unconnected with his functions, unless he consents in writing or the special chamber of the Ustavni Sud authorises it. Cases of flagrante delicto are reserved; authorisation must then be requested within twenty-four hours.

The special chamber weighs the free exercise of the mandate against the interest in prosecution. It refuses authorisation where the proceedings appear intended to prevent a member from taking part in a vote.

### Article 102 / Constituent peoples

The Confederation recognises seven constituent peoples:

a. the Slovenes (Slovenci);

b. the Croats (Hrvati);

c. the Serbs (Srbi);

d. the Bosniaks (Bošñaci);

e. the Montenegrins (Crnogorci);

f. the Macedonians (Makedonci);

g. the Bulgarians (Bãlgari).

Every citizen of the Confederation who has reached the age of eighteen may register in the national register of the people of his choice. This choice is personal and voluntary. No one may be compelled to declare a national affiliation or suffer prejudice by reason of that choice.

No one may register in another national register before a period of fifteen years has elapsed since his previous registration. Withdrawal from a register is free; it does not allow registration in another register before that period has elapsed.

A first registration is probationary for three years: it may be rectified once, in favour of another register. The rectification takes effect under the conditions of the following paragraph; the period of fifteen years runs from the rectification.

A registration or a change of register takes effect, for the election of the Narodni Sabor and eligibility to it, only from the first election held more than one year after the application.

Citizens who do not wish to register in any national register retain all of their civic rights. They take part in elections to the Kongres and in referendums but do not vote for the Narodni Sabor.

Where the Constitution refers to a person's belonging to a constituent people, it means his registration in the national register of that people.

## Chapter 2 / The Kongres

### Article 103 / Role and composition

The Kongres is the principal legislative organ of the Confederation.

It is composed of two hundred and fifty delegates elected by direct universal suffrage by the citizens of each State.

Each State forms one electoral constituency and receives at least two and at most twenty seats. Seats are distributed among the constituencies in proportion to their population by the Sainte-Laguë method, within these limits.

The confederal autonomous municipalities of Brčko and Mostar each form one constituency. The floor of two delegates does not apply to them, but each elects at least one delegate.

After each census, the Konfederalno Veće establishes the distribution of seats according to the resident population of each constituency. The new distribution applies from the next general election of the Kongres.

### Article 104 / Election of the Kongres

Delegates to the Kongres are elected by proportional list voting.

Elections are held every four years, on the first Sunday of October.

Every citizen of the Confederation aged eighteen or over and enjoying his civic rights is an elector. Every elector aged twenty-five or over is eligible.

### Article 105 / Competences of the Kongres

The Kongres:

a. enacts confederal laws;

b. adopts the confederal budget;

c. ratifies international treaties;

d. authorises the deployment of the army;

e. elects, jointly with the Narodni Sabor, the members of the Konfederalno Veće;

f. elects the judges of the Ustavni Sud on the proposal of the Narodni Sabor;

g. exercises general oversight over the Konfederalno Veće and the confederal administration;

h. grants pardons;

i. grants the guarantee to the constitutions of the States (Article 52);

j. holds the other elections assigned to it by the Constitution.

### Article 106 / Functioning of the Kongres

The Kongres elects from among its members a president and two vice-presidents. The three may not belong to the same constituent people.

The Kongres sits in ordinary session twice a year. Extraordinary sessions may be convened at the request of the Konfederalno Veće, of one quarter of the delegates, or of eight States.

Decisions of the Kongres are taken by simple majority of the delegates present, unless the Constitution provides otherwise.

The quorum is an absolute majority of the delegates.

### Article 106 bis / Initiative and promulgation of laws

The right of legislative initiative belongs:

a. to the Konfederalno Veće;

b. to each delegate to the Kongres;

c. to each national delegation of the Narodni Sabor;

d. to at least eight States, of which no more than half belong to the same constituent people (Article 1 bis).

Bills are tabled before the Kongres.

The President of the Confederation promulgates confederal laws and has them published in the Official Journal within thirty days of their final adoption. He may not oppose them.

## Chapter 3 / The Narodni Sabor (Assembly of Nations)

### Article 107 / Role and composition

The Narodni Sabor is the organ representing the constituent peoples of the Confederation.

It is composed of seven national delegations of ten members each, seventy members in total.

Each constituent people elects its ten representatives to the Narodni Sabor.

### Article 108 / Election of the Narodni Sabor

Members of the Narodni Sabor are elected by direct universal suffrage by the citizens registered in the corresponding national register.

Only citizens registered in the national register of a people are eligible for its delegation, under the conditions of Article 102.

The electoral constituency is the whole nation: all the Serbs of the Confederation, whatever their State of residence, vote together for the ten Serb representatives. The same applies to each constituent people.

Voting is by proportional list.

The election is held on the same day as that of the Kongres.

The term of office is four years.

### Article 109 / Competences of the Narodni Sabor

The Narodni Sabor exercises the following competences:

**a. Right of mandatory consent.** Confederal laws on the following matters may not enter into force without the approval of the Narodni Sabor:

- languages and the alphabet;
- education and national school curricula;
- culture and the protection of national heritage;
- the symbols of the Confederation and of the States;
- confederal memorial policy, including official commemorations, confederal public holidays, and the naming of institutions, infrastructure and places under the Confederation;
- changes to State borders;
- the status of autonomous entities;
- the rights of the constituent peoples;
- the list of confederal crimes and the competences of the confederal security forces;
- the confederal financial equalisation formula;
- constitutional amendments.

International treaties concerning these matters are subject to the same right of consent. In the event of refusal, the conciliation procedure of Article 110 ter applies.

**b. Alarm-bell procedure.** For any other confederal law, if two thirds of the members of a national delegation vote an alarm motion within thirty days of the adoption of the law by the Kongres, the law is suspended and referred to the conciliation committee (Article 110 ter). If conciliation fails, the law may be adopted only by a two-thirds majority of the Kongres.

**c. Nomination of candidates.** Each national delegation proposes one candidate to the Konfederalno Veće, three candidates for each seat of its people to be filled on the Ustavni Sud, and three candidates to the Confederal Court of Audit.

**d. Petitions of autonomous entities.** The Narodni Sabor receives and examines the petitions of autonomous entities.

**e. Advisory opinion.** The Narodni Sabor may issue an opinion on any matter of confederal interest.

### Article 110 / Functioning of the Narodni Sabor

The Narodni Sabor elects a president from among its members. The presidency rotates each year among the national delegations, in the following order: Slovene, Croat, Serb, Bosniak, Montenegrin, Macedonian, Bulgarian.

The national delegations meet separately on questions internal to their people and in plenary assembly on confederal questions.

Decisions of the Narodni Sabor in plenary assembly are taken by simple majority of the members present.

For matters subject to the right of mandatory consent, approval requires a simple majority in plenary assembly and a simple majority in at least five of the seven national delegations.

Whenever the Constitution requires a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations, the delegations forming that majority must also together represent a majority of the citizens enrolled in the national registers, according to the latest annual count of the Confederal Chancellery (Article 115 bis).

For constitutional amendments, approval requires a two-thirds majority in plenary assembly and a simple majority in at least six of the seven national delegations, or in each of the seven delegations for the entrenched guarantees (Article 150).

The Narodni Sabor decides on laws subject to its consent within ninety days of their transmission by the Kongres. Failing that, its consent is deemed to be given. Constitutional amendments always require an express vote.

### Article 110 bis / Qualification of laws

When transmitting an adopted law to the Narodni Sabor, the President of the Kongres states whether it falls under the right of mandatory consent (Article 109 a).

Any national delegation may challenge that qualification before the Ustavni Sud within thirty days of the transmission. The Ustavni Sud decides within thirty days.

The time limits laid down in Articles 109 and 110 are suspended while the proceedings are pending.

### Article 110 ter / Conciliation

The conciliation committee is composed of fourteen members: for each constituent people, one delegate appointed by the Kongres from among its members and one member appointed by the national delegation in the Narodni Sabor.

It is seized when the Narodni Sabor refuses its consent to a law or a treaty (Article 109 a), or when an alarm motion is adopted (Article 109 b). It deliberates only on the provisions in dispute.

Within sixty days, it adopts a single compromise text by a majority of its members. In the case of an alarm motion, that majority must include the vote of the member appointed by the national delegation that raised it.

The Kongres and the Narodni Sabor decide on the compromise text by a single vote, without amendment. For a law or treaty subject to consent, the Narodni Sabor decides by the majority laid down in Article 110.

If both chambers adopt the compromise, the law enters into force in that version. If conciliation fails:

a. a law or treaty subject to consent is abandoned;

b. a law subject to an alarm motion may be adopted only by a two-thirds majority of the Kongres;

c. the confederal budget is never abandoned: failing adoption by a two-thirds majority of the Kongres, the lowest amount among those submitted to the committee is deemed adopted for each disputed item.

## Chapter 4 / The Konfederalno Veće (Confederal Council)

### Article 111 / Role, composition and decisions

The Konfederalno Veće is the collegial executive organ of the Confederation.

It is composed of seven members, one per constituent people.

Decisions of the Konfederalno Veće are taken collegially by consensus.

When consensus cannot be reached, a decision requires the votes of at least four councillors whose peoples together represent a majority of the citizens enrolled in the national registers (Article 110), or the votes of at least five councillors. Where a seat is vacant, a majority of the members in office suffices; in the event of a tie, the serving president has the casting vote.

However, when a decision is adopted against the opinion of at least three councillors, its execution is suspended for thirty days. During that period, the Konfederalno Veće must hear the national delegations of the Narodni Sabor whose peoples are represented by the minority councillors.

At the end of that period, the Konfederalno Veće re-examines the decision. If the initial majority is confirmed, the decision becomes enforceable. If a new majority emerges in favour of another solution, that solution is adopted.

This procedure applies neither to decisions of day-to-day management, nor to decisions to deploy the confederal security forces against an imminent threat to human life, nor to the declaration of a state of emergency and the measures taken under Article 146.

The annual report of the Konfederalno Veće to the Kongres states, by department, the number of decisions adopted by consensus and the number adopted by majority.

### Article 112 / Election of the Konfederalno Veće

Each national delegation of the Narodni Sabor proposes one candidate for the office of confederal councillor of its nation. The candidate is elected by simple majority within his national delegation.

The candidate must have been registered in the national register of his people for at least eight years.

The Kongres and the Narodni Sabor, meeting as the Confederal Assembly, decide on each candidate by a separate vote, by simple majority.

If a candidate is rejected, his national delegation proposes a new candidate within thirty days. After a second rejection, it presents three candidates, from among whom the Confederal Assembly elects the councillor by relative majority.

Until his successor takes office, the outgoing councillor deals with current business.

The term of the Konfederalno Veće is four years. Its members may be re-elected once.

### Article 112 bis / Vacancy

If a seat on the Konfederalno Veće falls vacant, the national delegation concerned proposes a replacement within thirty days. He is elected under the procedure of Article 112 for the remainder of the term.

Until the replacement is elected, the Konfederalno Veće entrusts the direction of the department concerned to one of its members.

If the vacancy concerns the President of the Confederation, the presidency is held in the interim by the councillor who follows him in the order of rotation; the replacement completes the presidential year.

### Article 113 / The Confederal Presidency

The presidency of the Confederation is held in turn by each member of the Konfederalno Veće for one year.

The order of rotation is: Slovene, Croat, Serb, Bosniak, Montenegrin, Macedonian, Bulgarian. The cycle resumes at the end of the seven years.

The President of the Confederation chairs the meetings of the Konfederalno Veće and represents the Confederation abroad. He holds no powers superior to those of the other councillors, save the casting vote in the event of a tie.

The President may not serve two consecutive presidential terms.

### Article 114 / Confederal departments

Each member of the Konfederalno Veće heads a confederal department. The departments are:

a. Foreign Affairs (seat: Sarajevo);

b. Defence and Security (seat: Sofija);

c. Finance and the Treasury (seat: Beograd);

d. Justice and Fundamental Rights (seat: Zagreb);

e. Education, Culture and Sciences (seat: Sofija);

f. Economy and Infrastructure (seat: Beograd);

g. Environment, Territory and Resources (seat: Sarajevo).

The allocation of departments among the councillors is decided collegially within the Konfederalno Veće. No nation may head the same department for two consecutive terms.

Each councillor heads his department from its seat. Meetings of the Konfederalno Veće are held in Sarajevo.

### Article 115 / Incompatibilities

Members of the Konfederalno Veće may hold no other public office, elected mandate or private gainful activity during their term.

They may sit neither in the Kongres nor in the Narodni Sabor.

### Article 115 bis / Confederal Chancellery

The Confederal Chancellery (Konfederalna Kancelarija) is the general staff of the Konfederalno Veće. It is directed by the Chancellor of the Confederation (kancelar Konfederacije), elected by the Confederal Assembly by absolute majority on the proposal of the Konfederalno Veće, for a term of four years renewable once.

The Confederal Chancellery:

a. organises confederal elections and votes and records their results;

b. verifies the signatures of popular initiatives and referendum requests, as well as requests made by the States;

c. keeps the national registers, separately from the register of citizens (Article 14);

d. publishes the Official Journal.

Each year it publishes, for each national register, the total number of citizens enrolled on 1 January, which serves to calculate the double key (Articles 110 and 111), together with the number of registrations, rectifications, changes of register and withdrawals. Any national delegation may apply to the Ustavni Sud if it considers that concerted registrations distort the representation of its people; the Ustavni Sud may deprive the registrations concerned of effect for the following election.

## Chapter 5 / The Ustavni Sud (Constitutional Court)

### Article 116 / Role and composition

The Ustavni Sud is the supreme judicial organ of the Confederation in constitutional matters.

It sits in Zagreb.

It is composed of fourteen judges, two per constituent people.

It sits in plenary assembly or in two chambers of seven judges, each comprising one judge of each constituent people. One of them forms the special chamber provided for in Articles 118 and 144. The law distributes cases between the chambers and the plenary assembly.

### Article 117 / Appointment of judges

For each seat to be filled, the national delegation of the people concerned proposes three candidates.

The Kongres elects the judge from among the three candidates proposed, by a two-thirds majority. If no candidate obtains that majority after two rounds of voting, the Kongres elects him from among the same candidates by an absolute majority of its members.

Judges must be trained jurists with at least fifteen years of professional experience.

Candidates must have been registered for at least eight years in the national register of the people whose delegation proposes them.

The term of office is twelve years and is not renewable.

Half of the Ustavni Sud is renewed every six years, one judge per constituent people, in order to ensure the continuity of case law. At the first composition of the Court, one judge per people, designated by lot, serves a term of six years.

The outgoing judge remains in office until his successor takes office. In the event of a vacancy, the successor is elected under the same procedure for the remainder of the term.

### Article 118 / Competences of the Ustavni Sud

The Ustavni Sud:

a. rules on the conformity of confederal and State laws with the Constitution;

b. settles conflicts of competence between the Confederation and the States, and between States;

c. rules on individual complaints of violation of fundamental rights;

d. rules on disputes concerning the rights of autonomous entities;

e. rules on the validity of popular initiatives and referendums;

f. rules on the regularity of confederal elections;

g. rules on complaints of breach of financial equality and of manifest disproportion in confederal investment;

h. authorises and supervises, through a special chamber, investigations of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured targeting confederal or State elected officials, members of State governments and judges, or bearing on the competences of a State, as well as the collection measures of the Konfederalna Obavěštajna Agencija on the territory of the Confederation (Article 145 ter), and grants the authorisations provided for in Article 101 bis;

i. rules on complaints concerning the action of the confederal security forces and of the Konfederalna Obavěštajna Agencija which infringe fundamental rights or the rights of a constituent people, after the procedure of Article 138 bis;

j. rules on the qualification of laws with regard to the right of mandatory consent (Article 110 bis) and of revision proposals with regard to the entrenched guarantees (Article 150);

k. reviews the declaration and extension of a state of emergency and the measures taken under Article 146;

l. rules on complaints concerning the national registers (Article 115 bis);

m. finds failures of the States to comply with confederal law (Article 50 bis);

n. interprets the Constitution in the last instance.

Decisions of the Ustavni Sud are final and binding on all organs of the Confederation and of the States.

### Article 119 / Independence

The judges of the Ustavni Sud exercise their office in full independence. They may receive instructions from no organ of the Confederation, no State and no national delegation.

They may be removed only for serious misconduct, by decision of two thirds of the Kongres and two thirds of the Narodni Sabor.

## Chapter 5 bis / Confederal justice

### Article 119 bis / Confederal Court of Justice

The Confederal Court of Justice (Konfederalni Sud) is the supreme judicial authority of the Confederation, subject to the competences of the Ustavni Sud.

It hears:

a. appeals against decisions of State courts concerning the application of confederal law;

b. appeals against decisions of confederal authorities;

c. in the last instance, offences within the competence of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured (Article 141);

d. other cases assigned to it by law, where appropriate as the sole instance (Article 32).

The law sets the number of its judges and may establish confederal courts of first instance. The composition of the Court reflects the seven constituent peoples. Judges are elected by the Kongres for six years; they may be re-elected.

The judges of the Confederal Court of Justice exercise their office in full independence.

### Article 119 ter / Confederal Prosecutor's Office

The Confederal Prosecutor's Office (Konfederalno Tužilaštvo) conducts prosecutions for offences within the competence of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured (Article 141). The Bureau reports to it on its investigations; the Prosecutor's Office decides whether to prosecute.

The Prosecutor General of the Confederation is proposed by the Konfederalno Veće and elected by the Kongres by a two-thirds majority and by the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations, for a non-renewable term of seven years. He may not belong to the same people as the director-general of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured in office at the time of his election. The outgoing holder remains in office until his successor takes office.

The Confederal Prosecutor's Office receives instructions from no authority.

## Chapter 6 / Direct democracy

### Article 120 / Direct democracy in the States

Each State guarantees its citizens the exercise of direct democracy.

The citizens of each State vote directly on the laws of their State by referendum, in accordance with the constitution of the State.

Each State guarantees the right of popular initiative enabling its citizens to propose laws or amendments to the State constitution.

### Article 121 / Confederal popular initiative

Three hundred thousand citizens of the Confederation may, within eighteen months, propose the total or partial revision of the Confederal Constitution by way of popular initiative (Articles 148 and 149).

An initiative for partial revision is submitted to the vote of the people and of the States. It is adopted if it obtains a double majority: a majority of voters at confederal level and a majority of the States (each State counting as one vote, determined by the popular vote within that State).

### Article 122 / Optional confederal referendum

One hundred and fifty thousand citizens, or twelve States of which no more than half belong to the same constituent people (Article 1 bis), may request that any confederal law passed by the Kongres be submitted to the vote of the people.

They may make the same request, under the same conditions, for international treaties which:

a. are of unlimited duration and may not be terminated;

b. provide for accession to an international organisation;

c. contain important legislative provisions or whose implementation requires the adoption of confederal laws.

The request must be filed within one hundred days of the publication of the act.

The act is submitted to the popular vote and is rejected if a majority of voters vote against it.

### Article 123 / Mandatory referendum

The following are mandatorily submitted to the vote of the people and of the States:

a. amendments to the Confederal Constitution;

b. accession of the Confederation to collective security organisations or to supranational communities;

c. confederal laws declared urgent which lack a constitutional basis and whose period of validity exceeds one year (Article 123 bis).

Adoption requires a double majority: a majority of voters and a majority of the States.

### Article 123 bis / Urgent laws

A confederal law whose entry into force admits no delay may be declared urgent by the Kongres by an absolute majority of its members, with the approval of the Narodni Sabor by an absolute majority of its members and a simple majority in at least five of the seven national delegations. That approval replaces the alarm-bell procedure (Article 109 b). The law enters into force immediately. Its period of validity must be limited.

If a referendum is requested against an urgent law, it ceases to have effect one year after its adoption by the Kongres unless approved by the people within that period.

An urgent law which lacks a constitutional basis ceases to have effect one year after its adoption by the Kongres unless approved within that period by the people and the States (Article 123 c).

An urgent law which has not been approved in a vote may not be renewed.

### Article 124 / Double majority

Where the Constitution requires a double majority, the result of the popular vote in each State determines the vote of that State.

The confederal territories (Beograd, Sarajevo, Sofija, Zagreb) each count as one State.

The confederal autonomous municipalities (Brčko, Mostar) vote with the confederal territories geographically closest to them, unless the law provides otherwise.

## Chapter 7 / State parliaments

### Article 125 / Institutional autonomy of the States

Each State freely establishes its institutions in accordance with the Confederal Constitution.

Each State has a parliament (skupština) elected by direct universal suffrage and proportional representation.

Each State has a government responsible to its parliament.

Each State has independent courts.

### Article 126 / Symbols of the States

Each State freely adopts its flag, its coat of arms and its anthem.

The symbols of the State reflect the identity of the constituent people of which it is composed.

The flag of the State, the symbol of the constituent people and the flag of the Confederation are flown on the public buildings of each State.

### Article 127 / Languages of the States

Each State determines its official language within the regional South Slavic standard corresponding to its constituent people.

Autonomous entities within a State have the right to use the language of their community in local administration, education and justice.

# Title V / Languages and the alphabet

### Article 128 / National language

The national language of the Confederation is South Slavic (južnoslovenski jezik).

It is expressed through three regional standards:

a. the western standard (zapadni standard) — based on the Slovene vernaculars;

b. the central standard (srediñi standard) — based on the Štokavian vernaculars;

c. the eastern standard (istočni standard) — based on the Bulgarian-Macedonian vernaculars.

These three standards are equal in dignity and in law within the confederal institutions.

### Article 129 / Official alphabet

The official alphabet of the Confederation is the unified South Slavic Latin alphabet, composed of thirty-two letters:

A a · Ã ã · B b · C c · Č č · Ć ć · D d · Đ đ · E e · Ě ě · F f · G g · H h · I i · J j · K k · L l · Ļ ļ · M m · N n · Ñ ñ · O o · P p · R r · S s · Š š · T t · U u · V v · X x · Z z · Ž ž

The alphabet comprises seven vowels (a, ã, e, ě, i, o, u) and twenty-five consonants. The letter ě marks the yat in positions where its realisation differs between the regional standards; the letters ñ and ļ mark the palatal consonants /ɲ/ and /ʎ/ respectively; the letter x marks the affricate /dʒ/.

The letter ç does not belong to the unified South Slavic alphabet. Its use is reserved for the spelling of Albanian and Turkish proper names, in particular within the autonomous entities of those communities.

The Cyrillic alphabet remains in free use in the States that so wish. The Confederation guarantees the right to use the Cyrillic alphabet in public and private life.

The official acts of the Confederation are published in the unified South Slavic Latin alphabet and in the three regional standards.

The technical specification of the alphabet, the rules of toponymic transcription and the transition annex are set by confederal law, in accordance with the unified South Slavic Latin alphabet in its version in force.

### Article 130 / Linguistic freedom

No one may be compelled to use a regional standard or an alphabet other than the one of his choice.

The letter ě (yat) is read freely according to the speaker's regional standard — western, central or eastern. A common spelling entails no mandatory pronunciation: the same word written with ě may be realised according to the usage of each of the three standards. No confederal or State administration may impose a particular phonetic realisation of the yat, nor make any right conditional on the use of a given pronunciation.

The Confederation and the States encourage mutual understanding between speakers of the different standards and dialects.

The Confederation supports linguistic research and the promotion of dialectal diversity as the common heritage of the South Slavic peoples.

### Article 130 bis / Confederal Language Institute

The Confederal Language Institute (Konfederalni Institut za Jezik), an independent institution, has its seat in Ohrid.

It describes the South Slavic language in its three regional standards and fosters their mutual understanding. It prepares the specification of the alphabet, the rules of toponymic transcription and the official nomenclatures, which are laid down by confederal law (Article 129).

Its scientific council includes representatives of the seven constituent peoples and of the three regional standards.

The Institute may prescribe no pronunciation (Article 130).

# Title VI / Confederal finances and equalisation

## Chapter 1 / Financial equalisation

### Article 131 / Principle of equalisation

The Confederation ensures financial equalisation between its States in order to guarantee real equality of opportunity among citizens and the balanced development of the confederal territory.

The purpose of equalisation is to reduce disparities in financial capacity between States without removing the incentives for sound budgetary management.

No State may be excluded from the equalisation scheme on grounds related to the dominant national affiliation of its population.

### Article 132 / Equalisation formula

Confederal law sets the equalisation formula according to the following principles:

a. equalisation is calculated from objective and publicly verifiable indicators: population, gross domestic product per inhabitant, fiscal capacity, and indicators of need (density, geography, socio-demographic burdens);

b. no State may, after equalisation, have tax resources per inhabitant below eighty-five per cent of the confederal average;

c. no contributing State may be deprived, by way of equalisation, of more than thirty per cent of the surplus of its fiscal capacity over the confederal average;

d. the equalisation formula is revised every six years, after review by the Ustavni Sud of its conformity with constitutional principles;

e. any amendment to the equalisation formula is subject to the mandatory consent of the Narodni Sabor under Article 109.

### Article 133 / Financial transparency

The confederal department of Finance and the Treasury publishes each year a public report detailing, State by State, the financial flows between the Confederation and the States: confederal revenue collected, allocations paid, equalisation received or contributed.

Certified by the Confederal Court of Audit (Article 136 bis), this report is transmitted to the Kongres, the Narodni Sabor and the Ustavni Sud. It is made public in the three regional standards within forty-five days of the close of the financial year.

Any State, any autonomous entity and any citizen may bring a complaint for breach of financial equality before the Ustavni Sud if they consider that the recorded flows depart without justification from the statutory formula.

## Chapter 2 / Geographic proportionality of confederal investment

### Article 134 / Principle of geographic proportionality

Confederal investment in transport, communications, energy and research infrastructure, and in cultural facilities of confederal interest, is distributed according to the principle of geographic proportionality.

No State may be durably excluded from the major confederal investment programmes.

### Article 135 / Multi-year programming

For each multi-year investment programme, confederal law sets distribution targets:

a. among the three regional standards (western, central, eastern), according to a key that takes account of demographic weight and established needs;

b. among the geographic areas of the Confederation, taking care not to concentrate investment in the most densely populated territories alone.

Any multi-year investment programme above a threshold set by law is submitted to the Narodni Sabor for an opinion before its adoption by the Kongres.

### Article 136 / Complaint for manifest disproportion

Any State that considers itself wronged by the distribution of a confederal investment programme may refer the matter to the Ustavni Sud.

The Ustavni Sud may annul any investment programme whose geographic distribution appears manifestly disproportionate with regard to real needs, constitutional indicators and earlier multi-year commitments.

In the event of annulment, the Konfederalno Veće has one hundred and twenty days to propose a revised programme.

## Chapter 3 / Financial control

### Article 136 bis / Confederal Court of Audit

The Confederal Court of Audit (Konfederalni Računski Sud) is the independent organ for the control of the finances of the Confederation.

It:

a. audits the accounts and management of the Confederation, its administrations and the institutions it finances;

b. certifies the annual report on financial flows between the Confederation and the States (Article 133) and verifies the calculation of equalisation (Article 132);

c. audits the implementation of multi-year investment programmes (Articles 134 to 136).

It is composed of seven members, one per constituent people, elected by the Kongres from among three candidates proposed by each national delegation of the Narodni Sabor, by the majority laid down in Article 117, for a non-renewable term of nine years.

It addresses its reports to the Kongres and the Narodni Sabor and makes them public in the three regional standards.

# Title VII / Confederal security

## Chapter 1 / General provisions

### Article 137 / Principle of police subsidiarity

Public security is first and foremost a matter for the States. Each State has its own police, in accordance with Article 125; the same applies to the confederal territories.

The confederal security forces exercise only the competences expressly conferred on them by this Constitution. They may not take the place of State police forces in their community policing and ordinary public-order missions.

No confederal security force may be used for the political surveillance of citizens, nor base its action on the national, religious or linguistic affiliation, real or supposed, of any person.

### Article 138 / The two confederal security forces

The Confederation has two security forces, distinct in nature, supervising authority and missions:

a. the **Konfederalna Žandarmerija** (Confederal Gendarmerie), a territorial force with military status, under the supervision of the Department of Defence and Security (seat: Sofija). Within it, the **Granična Straža** (GS, Border Guard) carries out border and customs missions;

b. the **Konfederalni Istražni Ured** (Confederal Bureau of Investigation), an investigative agency with enumerated competences, under the supervision of the Department of Justice and Fundamental Rights (seat: Zagreb).

The two forces may not be placed under the authority of the same member of the Konfederalno Veće. Their direction, command and budgets are separate.

The Confederation also has an intelligence service without police powers, the Konfederalna Obavěštajna Agencija (Article 145 bis).

### Article 138 bis / Inspector General of the security forces

The Inspector General of the confederal security forces (generalni inspektor sigurnosnih snaga) investigates complaints concerning the action of the Konfederalna Žandarmerija, the Konfederalni Istražni Ured and the Konfederalna Obavěštajna Agencija. Any person may apply to him.

He may obtain any document, including classified documents, hear any officer and enter any premises of those services. Where he finds an offence, he refers the matter to the Confederal Prosecutor's Office or to the competent judicial authority of the State.

His findings on a complaint may be challenged before the Confederal Court of Justice (Article 119 bis).

He is proposed by the security oversight committee (Article 138 ter) and elected by the Kongres by a two-thirds majority and by the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations, for a non-renewable term of seven years. He receives instructions from no authority. The outgoing holder remains in office until his successor takes office.

He submits a public report each year to the security oversight committee.

### Article 138 ter / Security oversight committee

The Kongres and the Narodni Sabor form a joint security oversight committee (Komisija za Nadzor Sigurnosti) of fourteen members: for each constituent people, one delegate appointed by the Kongres from among its members and one member appointed by the national delegation in the Narodni Sabor.

The committee oversees the activity of the Konfederalna Žandarmerija, the Konfederalni Istražni Ured and the Konfederalna Obavěštajna Agencija. It has access to classified information; its members are bound by secrecy.

Those services and the Inspector General submit a report to it each year. It addresses a public report each year to the Kongres and the Narodni Sabor.

## Chapter 2 / The Konfederalna Žandarmerija

### Article 139 / Missions

The Konfederalna Žandarmerija is competent for:

a. the surveillance and policing of the external borders of the Confederation, carried out by the Granična Straža;

b. customs and the fight against cross-border trafficking, carried out by the Granična Straža;

c. the policing of transport routes, waterways and structures of confederal interest;

d. the protection of confederal infrastructure, buildings and personnel, including in the confederal territories;

e. the policing of the confederal autonomous municipalities (Brčko, Mostar);

f. the protection of the environment and of natural areas of confederal interest;

g. reinforcement of State police forces, at the express request of the State concerned and for a fixed period.

Except in the case provided for in point g, the Konfederalna Žandarmerija may not act on the territory of a State for ordinary police missions. During a state of emergency, however, an ordinance adopted under Article 146 may deploy it there without the request of the State, for an area and a period that the ordinance sets, with the approval of the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations. In that context it may not be used against political, associative or trade-union gatherings.

### Article 139 bis / Control of the external borders

The Granična Straža checks every person crossing the external borders of the Confederation, on entry and on exit. The entries and exits of foreign nationals are recorded.

It monitors the borders between crossing points; the law sets its staffing and technical means.

Every asylum application lodged at the border is recorded and examined in accordance with Article 99. No one may be returned in breach of Article 26.

The Confederation concludes readmission agreements with neighbouring States and States of origin.

No checks on persons take place at the boundaries between the confederated States, nor between a State and a confederal autonomous municipality.

### Article 140 / Status and organisation

The Konfederalna Žandarmerija is a corps with military status as regards its organisation, hierarchy and discipline. Its officers are called žandarmi; the officers of the Granična Straža are called stražari. It carries out its police missions under the functional authority of the Department of Defence and Security.

It is commanded by a commander and two deputy commanders, proposed by the Konfederalno Veće and confirmed by the Kongres by a two-thirds majority and by the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations, for a non-renewable term of seven years. Their term coincides neither with the electoral cycle of the Kongres nor with that of the board of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured. The outgoing holder remains in office until his successor takes office. They may be removed only for serious misconduct, by joint decision of two thirds of the Kongres and a majority in five of the seven delegations of the Narodni Sabor.

The Granična Straža is directed, under their authority, by a commander appointed by the Konfederalno Veće on the proposal of the commander of the Konfederalna Žandarmerija.

Its recruitment and its officer corps reflect the diversity of the population of the Confederation. No people may be durably over-represented in its command.

In each State, the action of the Konfederalna Žandarmerija is regularly reported to the authorities of that State. It submits a report each year to the security oversight committee (Article 138 ter).

### Article 140 bis / Specialised intervention unit

A specialised intervention unit is established within the Konfederalna Žandarmerija, responsible for counter-terrorism, hostage rescue and very high-risk interventions falling within confederal competence.

Its recruitment reflects the diversity of the population of the Confederation. It has no command or budget separate from those of the Konfederalna Žandarmerija.

Its deployment on the territory of a State requires either the request of that State or the existence of a threat falling under Article 139 or Article 141, and is immediately reported to the authorities of the State concerned and to the special chamber of the Ustavni Sud.

The unit may not be used for ordinary public-order missions nor against political, associative or trade-union gatherings.

## Chapter 3 / The Konfederalni Istražni Ured

### Article 141 / Nature and enumerated competence

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured is an investigative agency without community-policing competence. It performs no territorial patrolling function.

Its competence is strictly limited to the following offences:

a. offences against the security of the Confederation and its institutions;

b. terrorism;

c. organised crime extending over the territory of two or more States;

d. corruption of confederal officials and elected representatives, and misappropriation of confederal funds;

e. offences against the integrity of confederal elections and confederal referendums;

f. crimes against humanity and war crimes falling within the competence of the Confederation.

This list may be amended only by an amendment to the Constitution (Articles 110 and 123). No ordinary law may extend the competence of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured.

For any offence outside this list, the Konfederalni Istražni Ured has no competence and refers the matter to the State police forces.

### Article 142 / Operational forces

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured has its own officers empowered to make arrests and to conduct apprehension operations within its enumerated competence.

The use of these operational forces is limited to carrying out investigations falling under Article 141. They may not be used for maintaining public order, dispersing gatherings or any general police mission.

Any armed operation of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured on the territory of a State is immediately reported to the judicial authorities of that State.

### Article 143 / Direction

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured is directed by a board of three members: a director-general and two deputy directors. The three may not belong to the same constituent people.

The members of the board are proposed by the Konfederalno Veće and confirmed by the Kongres by a two-thirds majority and by the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations.

The office of director-general rotates among the constituent peoples, in the order set by Article 113. No people may hold the office of director-general for two consecutive terms.

The day-to-day direction of the Bureau rests with the director-general. However, the unanimous agreement of the board is required for:

a. opening an investigation falling under Article 144 (elected officials, State officials, judges, competences of a State);

b. any armed operation conducted by the operational forces of the Bureau.

The term of office of the board members is seven years and is not renewable. It does not coincide with the electoral cycle of the Kongres. In the event of a vacancy, the replacement is appointed by the same procedure for a full term. The outgoing member remains in office until his successor takes office.

No member of the board may be removed except for serious misconduct, by joint decision of two thirds of the Kongres and a majority in five of the seven delegations of the Narodni Sabor.

### Article 144 / Prior judicial review

Any investigation of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured targeting a confederal or State elected official, a member of a State government or a judge, or bearing directly on the competences of a State, requires the prior authorisation of a special chamber of the Ustavni Sud.

Any measure infringing liberties (search, interception, arrest other than in flagrante delicto) requires the authorisation of a judge.

The special chamber of the Ustavni Sud may at any time order the suspension of an investigation it finds to lack a legal basis or to be diverted from its purpose.

### Article 145 / Prohibitions and accountability

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured is prohibited from:

a. creating or keeping files based on the national, religious, political or trade-union affiliation of persons;

b. monitoring the lawful political, associative or trade-union activity of citizens;

c. investigating the national delegations of the Narodni Sabor in the exercise of their functions.

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured submits each year to the Narodni Sabor, to the Ustavni Sud and to the security oversight committee (Article 138 ter) a public report setting out, by category, the number of investigations opened, closed and referred.

Any national delegation may bring a matter before the special chamber of the Ustavni Sud if it considers that the Konfederalni Istražni Ured has exceeded its competence or infringed the rights of a constituent people.

## Chapter 3 bis / Intelligence

### Article 145 bis / Konfederalna Obavěštajna Agencija

The Konfederalna Obavěštajna Agencija (Confederal Intelligence Agency) collects and analyses the information necessary for the security of the Confederation. It has its seat in Sarajevo and comprises an external branch and an internal branch.

The external branch gathers intelligence abroad. It carries out no collection activity on the territory of the Confederation.

The internal branch acts only to prevent:

a. espionage and foreign interference;

b. terrorism;

c. the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction;

d. attacks on the critical infrastructure and information systems of the Confederation.

This list may be amended only by an amendment to the Constitution (Articles 110 and 123).

The Agency has no police powers: it may not arrest, search or question under compulsion. Where it discovers an offence, it informs the Konfederalni Istražni Ured or the police of the competent State.

### Article 145 ter / Direction and oversight of the Agency

The Agency is placed under the collegial authority of the Konfederalno Veće; no councillor directs it alone.

It is directed by a director and two deputy directors. They are proposed by the Konfederalno Veće and confirmed by the Kongres by a two-thirds majority and by the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations, for a non-renewable term of seven years. The outgoing holder remains in office until his successor takes office.

Any collection measure infringing privacy on the territory of the Confederation, in particular interception, surveillance or intrusion into information systems, requires the prior authorisation of the special chamber of the Ustavni Sud, for a limited and renewable period.

The prohibitions of Article 145 apply to the Agency. Data collected on a person are destroyed as soon as they are no longer necessary.

The Agency reports to the security oversight committee (Article 138 ter). The Inspector General of the security forces (Article 138 bis) investigates complaints concerning it.

## Chapter 4 / State of emergency

### Article 146 / State of emergency

In the event of a serious and imminent threat to the existence of the Confederation, its constitutional order or the safety of the population, or in the event of a major disaster, the Konfederalno Veće may declare a state of emergency over all or part of the territory.

The declaration is submitted within seven days to the Kongres and the Narodni Sabor, each of which confirms it by an absolute majority of its members. Failing confirmation, the state of emergency ends.

A state of emergency is declared for no more than ninety days. It may be extended for the same period under the same procedure.

During a state of emergency, the Konfederalno Veće may adopt by ordinance the measures strictly necessary to deal with the situation. Those ordinances cease to have effect when the state of emergency ends.

No measure may infringe human dignity (Article 8), the right to life or the prohibition of torture (Article 11), freedom of conscience and belief (Article 16) or the presumption of innocence (Article 32), nor make any distinction on grounds of national, religious or linguistic affiliation.

The Kongres and the Narodni Sabor may not be prevented from sitting. The term of elected confederal organs expiring during a state of emergency is extended until ninety days after its end.

The Ustavni Sud reviews the declaration, its extension and each of the measures taken. Matters may be brought before it by a national delegation, by one quarter of the delegates to the Kongres, by a State or by any person aggrieved. It rules on the declaration within seven days.

# Title VIII / Revision of the Constitution

### Article 147 / Principle

The Constitution may be revised totally or partially at any time.

Every revision is submitted to the vote of the people and of the States (Article 123). It enters into force as soon as it is accepted, unless otherwise provided.

When it originates with the authorities, a revision follows the legislative procedure. It requires the express consent of the Narodni Sabor by the majority laid down in Article 110 and, for the entrenched guarantees, by that laid down in Article 150.

No revision may violate the mandatory provisions of international law.

### Article 148 / Total revision

A total revision may be proposed by the people (Article 121), by the Kongres or by the Narodni Sabor.

Where the initiative comes from the people, or where the Kongres and the Narodni Sabor disagree, the people decide by a majority of voters whether a total revision is to be carried out.

If the people accept, the Kongres and the Narodni Sabor are re-elected in full. The newly elected chambers draft the new Constitution.

The draft is submitted to the consent of the Narodni Sabor, then to the vote of the people and of the States. Unless it retains the guarantees of Article 150 without restricting them, it requires the majority laid down in that Article.

### Article 149 / Partial revision

A partial revision may be requested by the people (Article 121) or decided by the Kongres.

Every partial revision must respect the principle of unity of subject matter. A popular initiative for partial revision takes the form of a specific draft.

The Ustavni Sud declares an initiative wholly or partly invalid if it fails to respect unity of subject matter or the mandatory provisions of international law (Article 118 e).

The Kongres and the Narodni Sabor recommend that an initiative be accepted or rejected. The Kongres may put forward a counter-proposal, which is put to the vote at the same time; the law regulates the vote on the initiative and the counter-proposal.

A popular initiative is not subject to the consent of the Narodni Sabor unless it affects an entrenched guarantee (Article 150).

### Article 150 / Entrenched guarantees

The following provisions may be revised only with the approval of the Narodni Sabor by a two-thirds majority in plenary assembly and a majority within each of the seven national delegations:

a. the parity of the constituent peoples in the Narodni Sabor and the Konfederalno Veće (Articles 107 and 111);

b. the right of mandatory consent, the alarm-bell procedure and conciliation (Articles 109 a and b, 110 and 110 ter);

c. human dignity, equality, the right to life and personal liberty (Articles 8, 9 and 11);

d. free enrolment in the national registers, the prohibition of any compulsion to declare one's national affiliation and the right to belong to no register (Article 102);

e. this Article.

This approval is required for any revision, total or partial, whether it originates with the people or with the authorities. It is in addition to acceptance by the people and the States. A popular initiative that affects an entrenched guarantee is submitted to the Narodni Sabor before the vote, within the time limit set by law; failing approval, it is not put to the vote.

When transmitting a revision proposal or a popular initiative to the Narodni Sabor, the President of the Kongres states whether it affects an entrenched guarantee. Any national delegation may challenge this statement before the Ustavni Sud under the conditions of Article 110 bis.

# Title IX / Final and transitional provisions

### Article 151 / Entry into force

This Constitution enters into force on the first day of the sixth month following its acceptance by the people and the States.

It is published in the three regional standards, whose versions are equally authentic (Articles 128 and 129).

### Article 152 / First elections

The first elections to the Kongres and the Narodni Sabor are held on the same day, no later than six months after this Constitution enters into force.

A conference of the governments of the States, meeting in Sarajevo, sets their date and organises them until the Confederal Chancellery takes office. It establishes the distribution of seats in the Kongres according to the latest census of each State (Article 103).

The first legislative term ends with the ordinary election on the first Sunday of October (Article 104) following the third anniversary of the first elections.

### Article 153 / First composition of the organs

The Confederal Assembly meets within thirty days of the first elections. It elects the members of the Konfederalno Veće and the Chancellor of the Confederation. The term of the first Konfederalno Veće ends with the first legislative term.

The first cycle of the presidencies of the Konfederalno Veće and of the Narodni Sabor begins with the Slovene people, in the order laid down in Articles 110 and 113.

The Ustavni Sud is constituted within six months of the first elections; one judge per people, chosen by lot, serves a term of six years (Article 117). The law likewise staggers the first terms of the governing board of the Confederal Central Bank, the Confederal Court of Audit and the commands of the security services, so that their renewals do not coincide.

Until the Konfederalno Veće takes office, the conference of the governments of the States conducts the current business of the Confederation.

### Article 154 / Opening of the national registers

The national registers open on the day this Constitution enters into force.

By way of derogation from Article 102, enrolments filed no later than sixty days before the first elections take effect for the election of the first Narodni Sabor and for eligibility to it.

For eight years after the opening of the registers, the length of enrolment required by Articles 112 and 117 is satisfied by an enrolment filed within six months of their opening and maintained without interruption in the same register.

### Article 155 / Existing law and conformity

The law in force on the territory of the Confederation when this Constitution enters into force remains applicable in so far as it is not contrary to it, until it is replaced by the competent confederal or State law.

The States bring their constitutions into line with this Constitution within two years and request their guarantee (Article 52).

The Confederation enacts the necessary implementing laws within three years, in particular on political rights, the national registers, the distribution of seats in the Kongres, financial equalisation and the symbols of the Confederation.

Transitional periods set by particular Articles, in particular Article 82, run from the entry into force of this Constitution.

### Article 156 / Finances and currency

The States continue to levy the general consumption taxes they levy when this Constitution enters into force until the confederal value added tax is introduced, no later than three years after that date (Article 99 sexies).

The States bring their legislation on direct taxes into line with the harmonisation law (Article 99 quinquies) within six years of its adoption.

The first equalisation formula (Article 132) is laid down by law within two years; it is revised for the first time six years after its entry into force.

The currencies that are legal tender on the territory of the Confederation when this Constitution enters into force remain so until they are replaced by the dinar (Article 97), on the conditions laid down by law and by the Confederal Central Bank.
