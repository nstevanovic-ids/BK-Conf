## Consolidated text, version 4 (13 October 2026)

*Published English text of the Constitution in force. The French working text is `docs/constitution/v4.md`; the transition annex is kept there.*

# Preamble

We, the peoples of the Balkan Confederation, and all the communities who share this land,

United by one language of a thousand faces which, from place to place, makes us mutually intelligible from the Julian Alps to the shores of the Black Sea, and holding this mutual understanding, older than our borders and our quarrels, as the first of our common goods;

Aware that we inhabit the one crossroads of Europe where the Latin West, the Byzantine East and the Ottoman world met, overlapped and mingled on the same soil, and recognising this overlap, long lived as a rupture, as our singularity;

Having each, in our turn and under different masters, known the subjection of empires, and knowing from an experience paid for in blood that no people is free while it dominates another;

Having each, in our turn, set the same refusal against empires, and built our nations not against empires alone, but against the very idea that one people may govern another without its consent;

Remembering those among us who made war on one another, and refusing both the oblivion that betrays the dead and the rancour that condemns the living;

Resolved to found our union neither on blood, nor on a supposed common origin, nor on the domination of the most numerous, but solely on the freely renewed will to live together;

Affirming that this Confederation is not one more empire, but its exact opposite: a home one enters by choice, remains in by consent, and that no one is compelled to inhabit;

Determined to guarantee to everyone their language, their faith, their memory and their letters, and to every person the right to belong to no one but themselves;

We give ourselves this Constitution.

*Naš dom — Our home.*

### Article 1 ter / Confederal capitals

The institutions of the Confederation are distributed among four capitals:

a. **Sarajevo** — political capital. Seat of the Kongres, the Narodni Sabor and the Konfederalno Veće. Sarajevo is the official capital of the Confederation and the residence of the Confederal Council.

b. **Beograd** — economic and financial capital. Seat of the Confederal Central Bank, the Confederal Stock Exchange, the Confederal Chamber of Commerce and the Confederal Department of Finance and the Treasury.

c. **Zagreb** — judicial capital. Seat of the Ustavni Sud (Constitutional Court), the Confederal Court of Justice and the Confederal Department of Justice and Fundamental Rights.

d. **Sofija** — capital of defence and sciences. Seat of the General Headquarters of the confederal armed forces, the Confederal Military Academy, the Confederal Academy of Sciences and the Confederal Department of Defence and Security. Sofija also hosts the seat of the Balkan Training Area (EBF).

No major confederal institution may be concentrated in a single city. Confederal law may assign secondary institutions to other cities of the Confederation.

Sessions of the Kongres may be held in any confederal territory by decision of the Konfederalno Veće.

## Chapter 2 / Autonomous entities

### Article 1 quater / Definition and status of autonomous entities

Within certain States, autonomous entities are constituted in order to guarantee the cultural, educational and municipal rights of minority communities.

Autonomous entities hold their own competences in matters of:

a. education in the minority language and adapted school curricula;

b. cultural institutions, media and community libraries;

c. management of ordinary municipal affairs within their territorial perimeter;

d. preservation and promotion of the cultural and religious heritage of the community;

e. cross-border cooperation with kindred communities in neighbouring States.

Autonomous entities have no legislative power of their own. They exercise their competences within the framework of the laws of the State to which they are attached and of the Confederal Constitution.

Autonomous entities have a right to be consulted on any decision of the State that directly affects the interests of their community.

### Article 1 sexies / Protection of autonomous entities

The territorial limits of an autonomous entity may not be altered without the consent of the majority of its inhabitants expressed by referendum.

Every State is bound to consult the autonomous entities it hosts before adopting measures that directly affect the education, culture or municipal organisation of their communities.

Autonomous entities may address petitions directly to the Narodni Sabor when they consider that their rights are threatened.

The Confederation ensures respect for the rights of autonomous entities and may intervene in the event of a proven violation.

### Article 1 septies / Confederal autonomous municipalities

The confederal autonomous municipalities of Brčko and Mostar are directly under confederal jurisdiction. They are attached to no State.

They have a municipal council elected by direct suffrage of their inhabitants.

The composition of their municipal council guarantees fair representation of all resident communities.

The Confederation finances their essential public services.

Their status may be amended only by a constitutional law approved by the Kongres, the Narodni Sabor and a local referendum.

# Title IV / Institutions of the Confederation

## Chapter 1 / General provisions

### Article 100 / Organs of the Confederation

The organs of the Confederation are:

a. the Kongres (legislative organ);

b. the Narodni Sabor (Assembly of Nations);

c. the Konfederalno Veće (Confederal Council — executive organ);

d. the Ustavni Sud (Constitutional Court — judicial organ).

### Article 101 / Separation of powers

The legislative, executive and judicial functions are separate.

No one may sit simultaneously in more than one confederal organ.

Members of the Konfederalno Veće may hold no other public office nor pursue any private gainful activity during their term.

### Article 102 / Constituent peoples

The Confederation recognises seven constituent peoples:

a. the Slovenes (Slovenci);

b. the Croats (Hrvati);

c. the Serbs (Srbi);

d. the Bosniaks (Bošñaci);

e. the Montenegrins (Crnogorci);

f. the Macedonians (Makedonci);

g. the Bulgarians (Bãlgari).

Every citizen of the Confederation registers freely in the national register of the people of his choice. This choice is personal, voluntary and revocable. No one may be compelled to declare a national affiliation or suffer prejudice by reason of that choice.

Citizens who do not wish to register in any national register retain all of their civic rights. They take part in elections to the Kongres and in referendums but do not vote for the Narodni Sabor.

## Chapter 2 / The Kongres

### Article 103 / Role and composition

The Kongres is the principal legislative organ of the Confederation.

It is composed of delegates elected by direct universal suffrage by the citizens of each State.

Each State forms one electoral constituency. The number of delegates per State is proportional to its population, with a floor of two delegates and a ceiling of twenty delegates.

The total number of delegates and their distribution among the States are set by confederal law after each census.

### Article 104 / Election of the Kongres

Delegates to the Kongres are elected by proportional list voting.

Elections are held every four years, on the first Sunday of October.

Every citizen of the Confederation aged eighteen or over and enjoying his civic rights is an elector. Every elector aged twenty-five or over is eligible.

### Article 105 / Competences of the Kongres

The Kongres:

a. enacts confederal laws;

b. adopts the confederal budget;

c. ratifies international treaties;

d. authorises the deployment of the army;

e. elects, jointly with the Narodni Sabor, the members of the Konfederalno Veće;

f. elects the judges of the Ustavni Sud on the proposal of the Narodni Sabor;

g. exercises general oversight over the Konfederalno Veće and the confederal administration;

h. grants pardons.

### Article 106 / Functioning of the Kongres

The Kongres elects from among its members a president and two vice-presidents. The three may not belong to the same constituent people.

The Kongres sits in ordinary session twice a year. Extraordinary sessions may be convened at the request of the Konfederalno Veće, of one quarter of the delegates, or of eight States.

Decisions of the Kongres are taken by simple majority of the delegates present, unless the Constitution provides otherwise.

The quorum is an absolute majority of the delegates.

## Chapter 3 / The Narodni Sabor (Assembly of Nations)

### Article 107 / Role and composition

The Narodni Sabor is the organ representing the constituent peoples of the Confederation.

It is composed of seven national delegations of ten members each, seventy members in total.

Each constituent people elects its ten representatives to the Narodni Sabor.

### Article 108 / Election of the Narodni Sabor

Members of the Narodni Sabor are elected by direct universal suffrage by the citizens registered in the corresponding national register.

The electoral constituency is the whole nation: all the Serbs of the Confederation, whatever their State of residence, vote together for the ten Serb representatives. The same applies to each constituent people.

Voting is by proportional list.

The election is held on the same day as that of the Kongres.

The term of office is four years.

### Article 109 / Competences of the Narodni Sabor

The Narodni Sabor exercises the following competences:

**a. Right of mandatory consent.** Confederal laws on the following matters may not enter into force without the approval of the Narodni Sabor:

- languages and the alphabet;
- education and national school curricula;
- culture and the protection of national heritage;
- the symbols of the Confederation and of the States;
- confederal memorial policy, including official commemorations, confederal public holidays, and the naming of institutions, infrastructure and places under the Confederation;
- changes to State borders;
- the status of autonomous entities;
- the rights of the constituent peoples;
- the list of confederal crimes and the competences of the confederal security forces;
- the confederal financial equalisation formula;
- constitutional amendments.

**b. Alarm-bell procedure.** For any other confederal law, if two thirds of the members of a national delegation vote an alarm motion within thirty days of the adoption of the law by the Kongres, the law is suspended and referred to a joint conciliation committee. If conciliation fails within sixty days, the law may be adopted only by a two-thirds majority of the Kongres.

**c. Nomination of candidates.** Each national delegation proposes one candidate to the Konfederalno Veće and three candidates to the Ustavni Sud for each of its two seats.

**d. Petitions of autonomous entities.** The Narodni Sabor receives and examines the petitions of autonomous entities.

**e. Advisory opinion.** The Narodni Sabor may issue an opinion on any matter of confederal interest.

### Article 110 / Functioning of the Narodni Sabor

The Narodni Sabor elects a president from among its members. The presidency rotates each year among the national delegations, in the following order: Slovene, Croat, Serb, Bosniak, Montenegrin, Macedonian, Bulgarian.

The national delegations meet separately on questions internal to their people and in plenary assembly on confederal questions.

Decisions of the Narodni Sabor in plenary assembly are taken by simple majority of the members present.

For matters subject to the right of mandatory consent, approval requires a simple majority in plenary assembly and a simple majority in at least five of the seven national delegations.

For constitutional amendments, approval requires a two-thirds majority in plenary assembly and a simple majority in at least six of the seven national delegations.

## Chapter 4 / The Konfederalno Veće (Confederal Council)

### Article 111 / Role, composition and decisions

The Konfederalno Veće is the collegial executive organ of the Confederation.

It is composed of seven members, one per constituent people.

Decisions of the Konfederalno Veće are taken collegially by consensus.

When consensus cannot be reached, the decision is taken by simple majority. In the event of a tie, the serving president has the casting vote.

However, when a decision is adopted against the opinion of at least three councillors, its execution is suspended for thirty days. During that period, the Konfederalno Veće must hear the national delegations of the Narodni Sabor whose peoples are represented by the minority councillors.

At the end of that period, the Konfederalno Veće re-examines the decision. If the initial majority is confirmed, the decision becomes enforceable. If a new majority emerges in favour of another solution, that solution is adopted.

This procedure applies neither to decisions of day-to-day management nor to emergency measures concerning the security of the Confederation.

The annual report of the Konfederalno Veće to the Kongres states, by department, the number of decisions adopted by consensus and the number adopted by majority.

### Article 112 / Election of the Konfederalno Veće

Each national delegation of the Narodni Sabor proposes one candidate for the office of confederal councillor of its nation. The candidate is elected by simple majority within his national delegation.

The Kongres and the Narodni Sabor, meeting as the Confederal Assembly, confirm the whole college of seven councillors by a single vote by simple majority.

If the college is rejected, the national delegations concerned by the rejection propose new candidates within thirty days.

The term of the Konfederalno Veće is four years. Its members may be re-elected once.

### Article 113 / The Confederal Presidency

The presidency of the Confederation is held in turn by each member of the Konfederalno Veće for one year.

The order of rotation is: Slovene, Croat, Serb, Bosniak, Montenegrin, Macedonian, Bulgarian. The cycle resumes at the end of the seven years.

The President of the Confederation chairs the meetings of the Konfederalno Veće and represents the Confederation abroad. He holds no powers superior to those of the other councillors, save the casting vote in the event of a tie.

The President may not serve two consecutive presidential terms.

### Article 114 / Confederal departments

Each member of the Konfederalno Veće heads a confederal department. The departments are:

a. Foreign Affairs (seat: Sarajevo);

b. Defence and Security (seat: Sofija);

c. Finance and the Treasury (seat: Beograd);

d. Justice and Fundamental Rights (seat: Zagreb);

e. Education, Culture and Sciences (seat: Sofija);

f. Economy and Infrastructure (seat: Beograd);

g. Environment, Territory and Resources (seat: Sarajevo).

The allocation of departments among the councillors is decided collegially within the Konfederalno Veće. No nation may head the same department for two consecutive terms.

Each councillor heads his department from its seat. Meetings of the Konfederalno Veće are held in Sarajevo.

### Article 115 / Incompatibilities

Members of the Konfederalno Veće may hold no other public office, elected mandate or private gainful activity during their term.

They may sit neither in the Kongres nor in the Narodni Sabor.

## Chapter 5 / The Ustavni Sud (Constitutional Court)

### Article 116 / Role and composition

The Ustavni Sud is the supreme judicial organ of the Confederation in constitutional matters.

It sits in Zagreb.

It is composed of fourteen judges, two per constituent people.

### Article 117 / Appointment of judges

For each of its two seats, each national delegation of the Narodni Sabor proposes three candidates.

The Kongres elects the two judges from among the three candidates proposed, by a two-thirds majority.

Judges must be trained jurists with at least fifteen years of professional experience.

The term of office is twelve years and is not renewable.

One third of the Court is renewed every four years in order to ensure the continuity of case law.

### Article 118 / Competences of the Ustavni Sud

The Ustavni Sud:

a. rules on the conformity of confederal and State laws with the Constitution;

b. settles conflicts of competence between the Confederation and the States, and between States;

c. rules on individual complaints of violation of fundamental rights;

d. rules on disputes concerning the rights of autonomous entities;

e. rules on the validity of popular initiatives and referendums;

f. rules on the regularity of confederal elections;

g. rules on complaints of breach of financial equality and of manifest disproportion in confederal investment;

h. authorises and supervises, through a special chamber, investigations of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured targeting confederal or State elected officials, members of State governments and judges, or bearing on the competences of a State;

i. rules on complaints concerning the action of the confederal security forces;

j. interprets the Constitution in the last instance.

Decisions of the Ustavni Sud are final and binding on all organs of the Confederation and of the States.

### Article 119 / Independence

The judges of the Ustavni Sud exercise their office in full independence. They may receive instructions from no organ of the Confederation, no State and no national delegation.

They may be removed only for serious misconduct, by decision of two thirds of the Kongres and two thirds of the Narodni Sabor.

## Chapter 6 / Direct democracy

### Article 120 / Direct democracy in the States

Each State guarantees its citizens the exercise of direct democracy.

The citizens of each State vote directly on the laws of their State by referendum, in accordance with the constitution of the State.

Each State guarantees the right of popular initiative enabling its citizens to propose laws or amendments to the State constitution.

### Article 121 / Confederal popular initiative

Three hundred thousand citizens of the Confederation may, within eighteen months, propose an amendment to the Confederal Constitution by way of popular initiative.

The initiative is submitted to the vote of the people and of the States. It is adopted if it obtains a double majority: a majority of voters at confederal level and a majority of the States (each State counting as one vote, determined by the popular vote within that State).

### Article 122 / Optional confederal referendum

One hundred and fifty thousand citizens or twelve States may request that any confederal law passed by the Kongres be submitted to the vote of the people.

The request must be filed within one hundred days of the publication of the law.

The law is submitted to the popular vote and is rejected if a majority of voters vote against it.

### Article 123 / Mandatory referendum

The following are mandatorily submitted to the vote of the people and of the States:

a. amendments to the Confederal Constitution;

b. accession of the Confederation to collective security organisations or to supranational communities;

c. confederal laws declared urgent which lack a constitutional basis and whose period of validity exceeds one year.

Adoption requires a double majority: a majority of voters and a majority of the States.

### Article 124 / Double majority

Where the Constitution requires a double majority, the result of the popular vote in each State determines the vote of that State.

The confederal territories (Beograd, Sarajevo, Sofija, Zagreb) each count as one State.

The confederal autonomous municipalities (Brčko, Mostar) vote with the confederal territories geographically closest to them, unless the law provides otherwise.

## Chapter 7 / State parliaments

### Article 125 / Institutional autonomy of the States

Each State freely establishes its institutions in accordance with the Confederal Constitution.

Each State has a parliament (skupština) elected by direct universal suffrage and proportional representation.

Each State has a government responsible to its parliament.

Each State has independent courts.

### Article 126 / Symbols of the States

Each State freely adopts its flag, its coat of arms and its anthem.

The symbols of the State reflect the identity of the constituent people of which it is composed.

The flag of the State, the symbol of the constituent people and the flag of the Confederation are flown on the public buildings of each State.

### Article 127 / Languages of the States

Each State determines its official language within the regional South Slavic standard corresponding to its constituent people.

Autonomous entities within a State have the right to use the language of their community in local administration, education and justice.

# Title V / Languages and the alphabet

### Article 128 / National language

The national language of the Confederation is South Slavic (južnoslovenski jezik).

It is expressed through three regional standards:

a. the western standard (zapadni standard) — based on the Slovene vernaculars;

b. the central standard (središnji standard) — based on the Štokavian vernaculars;

c. the eastern standard (istočni standard) — based on the Bulgarian-Macedonian vernaculars.

These three standards are equal in dignity and in law within the confederal institutions.

### Article 129 / Official alphabet

The official alphabet of the Confederation is the unified South Slavic Latin alphabet, composed of thirty-two letters:

A a · Ã ã · B b · C c · Č č · Ć ć · D d · Đ đ · E e · Ě ě · F f · G g · H h · I i · J j · K k · L l · Ļ ļ · M m · N n · Ñ ñ · O o · P p · R r · S s · Š š · T t · U u · V v · X x · Z z · Ž ž

The alphabet comprises seven vowels (a, ã, e, ě, i, o, u) and twenty-five consonants. The letter ě marks the yat in positions where its realisation differs between the regional standards; the letters ñ and ļ mark the palatal consonants /ɲ/ and /ʎ/ respectively; the letter x marks the affricate /dʒ/.

The letter ç does not belong to the unified South Slavic alphabet. Its use is reserved for the spelling of Albanian and Turkish proper names, in particular within the autonomous entities of those communities.

The Cyrillic alphabet remains in free use in the States that so wish. The Confederation guarantees the right to use the Cyrillic alphabet in public and private life.

The official acts of the Confederation are published in the unified South Slavic Latin alphabet and in the three regional standards.

The technical specification of the alphabet, the rules of toponymic transcription and the transition annex are set by confederal law, in accordance with the unified South Slavic Latin alphabet in its version in force.

### Article 130 / Linguistic freedom

No one may be compelled to use a regional standard or an alphabet other than the one of his choice.

The letter ě (yat) is read freely according to the speaker's regional standard — western, central or eastern. A common spelling entails no mandatory pronunciation: the same word written with ě may be realised according to the usage of each of the three standards. No confederal or State administration may impose a particular phonetic realisation of the yat, nor make any right conditional on the use of a given pronunciation.

The Confederation and the States encourage mutual understanding between speakers of the different standards and dialects.

The Confederation supports linguistic research and the promotion of dialectal diversity as the common heritage of the South Slavic peoples.

# Title VI / Confederal finances and equalisation

## Chapter 1 / Financial equalisation

### Article 131 / Principle of equalisation

The Confederation ensures financial equalisation between its States in order to guarantee real equality of opportunity among citizens and the balanced development of the confederal territory.

The purpose of equalisation is to reduce disparities in financial capacity between States without removing the incentives for sound budgetary management.

No State may be excluded from the equalisation scheme on grounds related to the dominant national affiliation of its population.

### Article 132 / Equalisation formula

Confederal law sets the equalisation formula according to the following principles:

a. equalisation is calculated from objective and publicly verifiable indicators: population, gross domestic product per inhabitant, fiscal capacity, and indicators of need (density, geography, socio-demographic burdens);

b. no State may, after equalisation, have tax resources per inhabitant below eighty-five per cent of the confederal average;

c. no contributing State may be deprived, by way of equalisation, of more than thirty per cent of the surplus of its fiscal capacity over the confederal average;

d. the equalisation formula is revised every six years, after review by the Ustavni Sud of its conformity with constitutional principles;

e. any amendment to the equalisation formula is subject to the mandatory consent of the Narodni Sabor under Article 109.

### Article 133 / Financial transparency

The confederal department of Finance and the Treasury publishes each year a public report detailing, State by State, the financial flows between the Confederation and the States: confederal revenue collected, allocations paid, equalisation received or contributed.

This report is transmitted to the Kongres, the Narodni Sabor and the Ustavni Sud. It is made public in the three regional standards within forty-five days of the close of the financial year.

Any State, any autonomous entity and any citizen may bring a complaint for breach of financial equality before the Ustavni Sud if they consider that the recorded flows depart without justification from the statutory formula.

## Chapter 2 / Geographic proportionality of confederal investment

### Article 134 / Principle of geographic proportionality

Confederal investment in transport, communications, energy and research infrastructure, and in cultural facilities of confederal interest, is distributed according to the principle of geographic proportionality.

No State may be durably excluded from the major confederal investment programmes.

### Article 135 / Multi-year programming

For each multi-year investment programme, confederal law sets distribution targets:

a. among the three regional standards (western, central, eastern), according to a key that takes account of demographic weight and established needs;

b. among the geographic areas of the Confederation, taking care not to concentrate investment in the most densely populated territories alone.

Any multi-year investment programme above a threshold set by law is submitted to the Narodni Sabor for an opinion before its adoption by the Kongres.

### Article 136 / Complaint for manifest disproportion

Any State that considers itself wronged by the distribution of a confederal investment programme may refer the matter to the Ustavni Sud.

The Ustavni Sud may annul any investment programme whose geographic distribution appears manifestly disproportionate with regard to real needs, constitutional indicators and earlier multi-year commitments.

In the event of annulment, the Konfederalno Veće has one hundred and twenty days to propose a revised programme.

# Title VII / Confederal security

## Chapter 1 / General provisions

### Article 137 / Principle of police subsidiarity

Public security is first and foremost a matter for the States. Each State has its own police, in accordance with Article 125.

The confederal security forces exercise only the competences expressly conferred on them by this Constitution. They may not take the place of State police forces in their community policing and ordinary public-order missions.

No confederal security force may be used for the political surveillance of citizens, nor base its action on the national, religious or linguistic affiliation, real or supposed, of any person.

### Article 138 / The two confederal security forces

The Confederation has two security forces, distinct in nature, supervising authority and missions:

a. the **Konfederalna Žandarmerija** (Confederal Gendarmerie), a territorial force with military status, under the supervision of the Department of Defence and Security (seat: Sofija). Within it, the **Granična Straža** (GS, Border Guard) carries out border and customs missions;

b. the **Konfederalni Istražni Ured** (Confederal Bureau of Investigation), an investigative agency with enumerated competences, under the supervision of the Department of Justice and Fundamental Rights (seat: Zagreb).

The two forces may not be placed under the authority of the same member of the Konfederalno Veće. Their direction, command and budgets are separate.

## Chapter 2 / The Konfederalna Žandarmerija

### Article 139 / Missions

The Konfederalna Žandarmerija is competent for:

a. the surveillance and policing of the external borders of the Confederation, carried out by the Granična Straža;

b. customs and the fight against cross-border trafficking, carried out by the Granična Straža;

c. the policing of transport routes, waterways and structures of confederal interest;

d. the protection of confederal infrastructure, buildings and personnel;

e. the policing of the confederal territories (Beograd, Sarajevo, Sofija, Zagreb) and of the confederal autonomous municipalities (Brčko, Mostar);

f. the protection of the environment and of natural areas of confederal interest;

g. reinforcement of State police forces, at the express request of the State concerned and for a fixed period.

Except in the case provided for in point g, the Konfederalna Žandarmerija may not act on the territory of a State for ordinary police missions.

### Article 140 / Status and organisation

The Konfederalna Žandarmerija is a corps with military status as regards its organisation, hierarchy and discipline. Its officers are called žandarmi; the officers of the Granična Straža are called stražari. It carries out its police missions under the functional authority of the Department of Defence and Security.

Its recruitment and its officer corps reflect the composition of the seven constituent peoples. No people may be durably over-represented in its command.

In each State, the action of the Konfederalna Žandarmerija is regularly reported to the authorities of that State.

### Article 140 bis / Specialised intervention unit

A specialised intervention unit is established within the Konfederalna Žandarmerija, responsible for counter-terrorism, hostage rescue and very high-risk interventions falling within confederal competence.

Its recruitment reflects the seven constituent peoples. It has no command or budget separate from those of the Konfederalna Žandarmerija.

Its deployment on the territory of a State requires either the request of that State or the existence of a threat falling under Article 139 or Article 141, and is immediately reported to the authorities of the State concerned and to the special chamber of the Ustavni Sud.

The unit may not be used for ordinary public-order missions nor against political, associative or trade-union gatherings.

## Chapter 3 / The Konfederalni Istražni Ured

### Article 141 / Nature and enumerated competence

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured is an investigative agency without community-policing competence. It performs no territorial patrolling function.

Its competence is strictly limited to the following offences:

a. offences against the security of the Confederation and its institutions;

b. terrorism;

c. organised crime extending over the territory of two or more States;

d. corruption of confederal officials and elected representatives, and misappropriation of confederal funds;

e. offences against the integrity of confederal elections and confederal referendums;

f. crimes against humanity and war crimes falling within the competence of the Confederation.

This list may be amended only by a constitutional law subject to the mandatory consent of the Narodni Sabor. No ordinary law may extend the competence of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured.

For any offence outside this list, the Konfederalni Istražni Ured has no competence and refers the matter to the State police forces.

### Article 142 / Operational forces

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured has its own officers empowered to make arrests and to conduct apprehension operations within its enumerated competence.

The use of these operational forces is limited to carrying out investigations falling under Article 141. They may not be used for maintaining public order, dispersing gatherings or any general police mission.

Any armed operation of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured on the territory of a State is immediately reported to the judicial authorities of that State.

### Article 143 / Direction

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured is directed by a board of three members: a director-general and two deputy directors. The three may not belong to the same constituent people.

The members of the board are proposed by the Konfederalno Veće and confirmed by the Kongres by a two-thirds majority and by the Narodni Sabor by a majority in at least five of the seven national delegations.

The office of director-general rotates among the constituent peoples, in the order set by Article 113. No people may hold the office of director-general for two consecutive terms.

The day-to-day direction of the Bureau rests with the director-general. However, the unanimous agreement of the board is required for:

a. opening an investigation falling under Article 144 (elected officials, State officials, judges, competences of a State);

b. any armed operation conducted by the operational forces of the Bureau.

The term of office of the board members is seven years and is not renewable. It does not coincide with the electoral cycle of the Kongres. In the event of a vacancy, the replacement is appointed by the same procedure for a full term.

No member of the board may be removed except for serious misconduct, by joint decision of two thirds of the Kongres and a majority in five of the seven delegations of the Narodni Sabor.

### Article 144 / Prior judicial review

Any investigation of the Konfederalni Istražni Ured targeting a confederal or State elected official, a member of a State government or a judge, or bearing directly on the competences of a State, requires the prior authorisation of a special chamber of the Ustavni Sud.

Any measure infringing liberties (search, interception, arrest other than in flagrante delicto) requires the authorisation of a judge.

The special chamber of the Ustavni Sud may at any time order the suspension of an investigation it finds to lack a legal basis or to be diverted from its purpose.

### Article 145 / Prohibitions and accountability

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured is prohibited from:

a. creating or keeping files based on the national, religious, political or trade-union affiliation of persons;

b. monitoring the lawful political, associative or trade-union activity of citizens;

c. investigating the national delegations of the Narodni Sabor in the exercise of their functions.

The Konfederalni Istražni Ured submits each year to the Narodni Sabor and to the Ustavni Sud a public report setting out, by category, the number of investigations opened, closed and referred.

Any national delegation may bring a matter before the special chamber of the Ustavni Sud if it considers that the Konfederalni Istražni Ured has exceeded its competence or infringed the rights of a constituent people.
